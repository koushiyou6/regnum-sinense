[§4] Shiji IV: Biographies

魏公子列传：魏公子无忌者，魏昭王子少子而魏安釐王异母弟也。昭王薨，安釐王即位，封公子为信陵君。是时范睢亡魏相秦，以怨魏齐故，秦兵围大梁，破魏华阳下军，走芒卯。魏王及公子患之。

Biography of the Prince of Wei: Wuji, Prince1 of Wei, was the youngest son of King Zhao of Wei and the half-brother of King Anxi of Wei. When King Zhao died and King Anxi came to the throne, he enfeoffed the Prince as Lord Xinling. At this time Fan Ju had fled from Wei and become Chancellor of Qin, and because of his grudge against Wei Qi the Qin troops besieged Daliang, defeated the Wei army below Huayang, and put Mang Mao to flight. The King of Wei and the Prince were troubled by this.

公子为人仁而下士，士无贤不肖皆谦而礼交之，不敢以其富贵骄士。士以此方数千里争往归之，致食客三千人。当是时，诸侯以公子贤，多客，不敢加兵谋魏十馀年。

The Prince was by nature humane and humbled himself before gentlemen; whether a gentleman was worthy or unworthy, he met him with modesty and courtesy, and never dared to lord it over gentlemen on account of his wealth and rank. For this reason gentlemen from thousands of li around vied to go over to him, until he had three thousand retainers. At that time the Lords, because the Prince was worthy and had many retainers, dared not raise troops or plot against Wei for more than ten years.

公子与魏王博，而北境传举烽，言“赵寇至，且入界”。魏王释博，欲召大臣谋。公子止王曰：“赵王田猎耳，非为寇也。”复博如故。王恐，心不在博。居顷，复从北方来传言曰：“赵王猎耳，非为寇也。”魏王大惊，曰：“公子何以知之？”公子曰：“臣之客有能深得赵王阴事者，赵王所为，客辄以报臣，臣以此知之。”是後魏王畏公子之贤能，不敢任公子以国政。

The Prince was playing at bo2 with the King of Wei when from the northern border beacons were passed along, with word that "Zhao raiders have come and are about to cross the border". The King of Wei put down the game and wished to summon the great ministers to take counsel. The Prince stopped the King: "The King of Zhao is only out hunting; it is no raid." And he went on with the game as before. The King was afraid, and his mind was not on the game. After a while word came again from the north: "The King of Zhao was only hunting; it was no raid." The King of Wei was greatly startled and said: "How did the Prince know it?" The Prince said: "Among your servant's retainers is one who can get at the King of Zhao's secret doings; whatever the King of Zhao does, the retainer at once reports to your servant, and that is how I knew it." After this the King of Wei stood in fear of the Prince's worth and ability, and dared not entrust the Prince with the government of the state.

魏有隐士曰侯嬴，年七十，家贫，为大梁夷门监者。公子闻之，往请，欲厚遗之。不肯受，曰：“臣脩身絜行数十年，终不以监门困故而受公子财。”公子於是乃置酒大会宾客。坐定，公子从车骑，虚左，自迎夷门侯生。侯生摄敝衣冠，直上载公子上坐，不让，欲以观公子。公子执辔愈恭。侯生又谓公子曰：“臣有客在市屠中，原枉车骑过之。”公子引车入巿，侯生下见其客朱亥，俾倪故久立，与其客语，微察公子。公子颜色愈和。当是时，魏将相宗室宾客满堂，待公子举酒。巿人皆观公子执辔。从骑皆窃骂侯生。侯生视公子色终不变，乃谢客就车。至家，公子引侯生坐上坐，遍赞宾客，宾客皆惊。酒酣，公子起，为寿侯生前。侯生因谓公子曰：“今日嬴之为公子亦足矣。嬴乃夷门抱关者也，而公子亲枉车骑，自迎嬴於众人广坐之中，不宜有所过，今公子故过之。然嬴欲就公子之名，故久立公子车骑巿中，过客以观公子，公子愈恭。巿人皆以嬴为小人，而以公子为长者能下士也。”於是罢酒，侯生遂为上客。

In Wei there was a recluse called Hou Ying, seventy years old and poor, who was the keeper of the Yi Gate of Daliang. The Prince heard of him, went to call on him, and wished to make him a rich gift. He would not accept it, saying: "For some tens of years your servant has cultivated his person and kept his conduct clean; I will not, because of the hardship of keeping a gate, accept the Prince's wealth." The Prince thereupon set out wine and called a great gathering of his guests. When they were seated, the Prince went out with his carriage and horsemen, leaving the left-hand seat empty, to fetch Master Hou of the Yi Gate in person. Master Hou gathered up his worn robe and cap, went straight up and took the upper seat in the Prince's carriage without demurring, meaning to observe the Prince. The Prince held the reins with ever greater respect. Master Hou then said to the Prince: "I have a friend among the butchers of the market; I would have you trouble your carriage and horsemen to go round by him." The Prince drove the carriage into the market3. Master Hou got down to see his friend Zhu Hai, and, glancing sidelong, stood there on purpose a long while, talking with his friend and covertly watching the Prince. The Prince's expression grew ever milder. At that moment the generals, ministers, royal kinsmen and guests of Wei filled the hall, waiting for the Prince to raise the wine. The people of the market all watched the Prince holding the reins, and the attendant horsemen all cursed Master Hou under their breath. When Master Hou saw that the Prince's expression never changed, he took leave of his friend and got into the carriage. On reaching the house the Prince led Master Hou to the upper seat and presented him to the guests one by one, and the guests were all astonished. When the wine was flowing the Prince rose and drank Master Hou's health before him. Master Hou then said to the Prince: "What Ying has done for the Prince today is enough. Ying is a mere keeper of the bar at the Yi Gate, yet the Prince in person troubled his carriage and horsemen to fetch Ying himself, before a great company of men. It was not fitting to go out of the way for anyone, yet today the Prince went out of his way on purpose. Still, Ying wished to make the Prince's reputation complete, and so he kept the Prince's carriage and horsemen standing a long while in the market, calling on a friend in order to observe the Prince; and the Prince grew ever more respectful. The people of the market all take Ying for a petty man, and the Prince for an elder who can humble himself before gentlemen." With that the drinking ended, and Master Hou became the chief guest.

侯生谓公子曰：“臣所过屠者硃亥，此子贤者，世莫能知，故隐屠间耳。”公子往数请之，硃亥故不复谢，公子怪之。

Master Hou said to the Prince: "The butcher Zhu Hai, on whom I called, is a worthy man whom the world has not been able to recognise, and so he hides himself among the butchers." The Prince went several times to call on him, but Zhu Hai purposely never returned the courtesy, and the Prince thought it strange.

魏安釐王二十年，秦昭王已破赵长平军，又进兵围邯郸。公子姊为赵惠文王弟平原君夫人，数遗魏王及公子书，请救於魏。魏王使将军晋鄙将十万众救赵。秦王使使者告魏王曰：“吾攻赵旦暮且下，而诸侯敢救者，已拔赵，必移兵先击之。”魏王恐，使人止晋鄙，留军壁鄴，名为救赵，实持两端以观望。平原君使者冠盖相属於魏，让魏公子曰：“胜所以自附为婚姻者，以公子之高义，为能急人之困。今邯郸旦暮降秦而魏救不至，安在公子能急人之困也！且公子纵轻胜，弃之降秦，独不怜公子姊邪？”公子患之，数请魏王，及宾客辩士说王万端。魏王畏秦，终不听公子。公子自度终不能得之於王，计不独生而令赵亡，乃请宾客，约车骑百馀乘，欲以客往赴秦军，与赵俱死。

In the twentieth year of King Anxi of Wei4, King Zhao of Qin, having already destroyed the Zhao army at Changping, advanced his troops again and besieged Handan. The Prince's elder sister was the wife of Lord Pingyuan, the younger brother of King Huiwen of Zhao, and she sent letter after letter to the King of Wei and the Prince asking for relief from Wei. The King of Wei sent General Jin Bi at the head of a hundred thousand men to relieve Zhao. The King of Qin sent an envoy to tell the King of Wei: "I am attacking Zhao, and it will fall any day; should any of the Lords dare to relieve it, once I have taken Zhao I shall certainly move my troops and strike him first." The King of Wei was afraid and sent men to halt Jin Bi, who kept his army encamped at Ye, relieving Zhao in name but in fact holding to both sides and watching how things went. Lord Pingyuan's envoys came to Wei one after another, cap after canopy, and he reproached the Prince of Wei: "The reason Sheng5 attached himself to you by marriage was that the Prince's lofty sense of right could hasten to a man in his extremity. Now Handan may surrender to Qin any day and Wei's relief does not come — where is the Prince's hastening to a man in his extremity! And even if the Prince thinks nothing of Sheng and abandons him to surrender to Qin, has he no pity for his own sister?" The Prince was troubled; he pleaded with the King of Wei again and again, and his guests and disputants argued with the King in ten thousand ways. The King of Wei feared Qin and in the end would not listen to the Prince. The Prince judged that in the end he would never obtain it from the King, and resolved not to live on alone while Zhao perished; so he called on his guests, mustered a hundred-odd carriages and horsemen, and meant to go with his guests and fling himself on the Qin army, to die together with Zhao.

行过夷门，见侯生，具告所以欲死秦军状。辞决而行，侯生曰：“公子勉之矣，老臣不能从。”公子行数里，心不快，曰：“吾所以待侯生者备矣，天下莫不闻，今吾且死而侯生曾无一言半辞送我，我岂有所失哉？”复引车还，问侯生。侯生笑曰：“臣固知公子之还也。”曰：“公子喜士，名闻天下。今有难，无他端而欲赴秦军，譬若以肉投馁虎，何功之有哉？尚安事客？然公子遇臣厚，公子往而臣不送，以是知公子恨之复返也。”公子再拜，因问。侯生乃屏人间语，曰：“嬴闻晋鄙之兵符常在王卧内，而如姬最幸，出入王卧内，力能窃之。嬴闻如姬父为人所杀，如姬资之三年，自王以下欲求报其父仇，莫能得。如姬为公子泣，公子使客斩其仇头，敬进如姬。如姬之欲为公子死，无所辞，顾未有路耳。公子诚一开口请如姬，如姬必许诺，则得虎符夺晋鄙军，北救赵而西卻秦，此五霸之伐也。”公子从其计，请如姬。如姬果盗晋鄙兵符与公子。

On his way he passed the Yi Gate and saw Master Hou, and told him in full how he meant to die against the Qin army. He took his leave and set off, and Master Hou said: "May the Prince do his utmost; your old servant cannot follow." The Prince had gone several li when he grew uneasy at heart and said: "The way I have treated Master Hou was complete, and none under Heaven has not heard of it; now I am going to my death and Master Hou has not a word or half a phrase to send me off with — have I failed in something?" He turned the carriage back and questioned Master Hou. Master Hou laughed and said: "I knew, of course, that the Prince would come back." He said: "The Prince delights in gentlemen, and his name is heard throughout all under Heaven. Now there is trouble, and with no other plan you mean to fling yourself on the Qin army; that is like throwing meat to a starving tiger — what good can it do? And what use then are your retainers? Yet the Prince has treated me generously, and when the Prince went and I did not see him off, I knew by that that the Prince would resent it and come back." The Prince bowed twice and asked his counsel. Master Hou then sent the others away and spoke with him privately: "Ying has heard that the tally for Jin Bi's army is always kept in the King's bedchamber, and that Lady Ru is the most favoured and goes in and out of the King's bedchamber; it is in her power to steal it. Ying has heard that Lady Ru's father was killed by someone, and that Lady Ru nursed the grievance three years; from the King downward they sought to avenge her father, and none could. Lady Ru wept before the Prince, and the Prince had a retainer cut off her enemy's head and present it respectfully to Lady Ru. Lady Ru's wish to die for the Prince is one she would not refuse; only she has had no way to do it. If the Prince will but open his mouth once and ask Lady Ru, Lady Ru will surely consent; then you will have the tiger tally and can seize Jin Bi's army, go north to relieve Zhao and westward drive back Qin — that is a feat of the Five Hegemons." The Prince followed his plan and asked Lady Ru. Lady Ru did indeed steal Jin Bi's army tally and give it to the Prince.

公子行，侯生曰：“将在外，主令有所不受，以便国家。公子即合符，而晋鄙不授公子兵而复请之，事必危矣。臣客屠者硃亥可与俱，此人力士。晋鄙听，大善；不听，可使击之。”於是公子泣。侯生曰：“公子畏死邪？何泣也？”公子曰：“晋鄙嚄唶宿将，往恐不听，必当杀之，是以泣耳，岂畏死哉？”於是公子请硃亥。硃亥笑曰：“臣乃市井鼓刀屠者，而公子亲数存之，所以不报谢者，以为小礼无所用。今公子有急，此乃臣效命之秋也。”遂与公子俱。公子过谢侯生。侯生曰：“臣宜从，老不能。请数公子行日，以至晋鄙军之日，北乡自刭，以送公子。”公子遂行。

As the Prince set out, Master Hou said: "When a general is in the field, there are commands of his ruler that he does not accept, for the good of the state. If the Prince matches the tallies and Jin Bi does not hand the army over to the Prince but sends to ask the King again, the affair will surely be in danger. My friend the butcher Zhu Hai can go with you; he is a man of strength. If Jin Bi listens, very good; if he does not, you can have him strike him down." At this the Prince wept. Master Hou said: "Is the Prince afraid to die? Why do you weep?" The Prince said: "Jin Bi is a blustering old general; I fear that when I go he will not listen, and then I must kill him. That is why I weep — how could I be afraid to die?" The Prince then asked Zhu Hai. Zhu Hai laughed and said: "I am a butcher who plies the knife in the market-place, yet the Prince has in person called on me several times. The reason I did not return the courtesy was that I thought small civilities of no use. Now the Prince is in urgent need; this is the season for me to give my life." So he went with the Prince. The Prince went by to take leave of Master Hou. Master Hou said: "I ought to follow you, but I am old and cannot. Let me count the days of the Prince's journey, and on the day you reach Jin Bi's army I shall face north and cut my throat, to send the Prince off." The Prince then set out.

至鄴，矫魏王令代晋鄙。晋鄙合符，疑之，举手视公子曰：“今吾拥十万之众，屯於境上，国之重任，今单车来代之，何如哉？”欲无听。硃亥袖四十斤铁椎，椎杀晋鄙，公子遂将晋鄙军。勒兵下令军中曰：“父子俱在军中，父归；兄弟俱在军中，兄归；独子无兄弟，归养。”得选兵八万人，进兵击秦军。秦军解去，遂救邯郸，存赵。赵王及平原君自迎公子於界，平原君负籣矢为公子先引。赵王再拜曰：“自古贤人未有及公子者也。”当此之时，平原君不敢自比於人。公子与侯生决，至军，侯生果北乡自刭。

He reached Ye and, feigning a command of the King of Wei, took over from Jin Bi. Jin Bi matched the tallies but doubted him; raising his hand, he looked at the Prince and said: "I now hold a host of a hundred thousand, camped on the border — a weighty charge of the state. Now you come in a single carriage to replace me; how is this?" He meant not to obey. Zhu Hai, with an iron mace of forty jin in his sleeve, struck Jin Bi dead, and the Prince took command of Jin Bi's army. He drew up the troops and gave orders throughout the army: "Where father and son are both in the army, let the father go home; where elder and younger brother are both in the army, let the elder go home; an only son with no brothers, let him go home and support his parents." He obtained eighty thousand picked men and advanced to attack the Qin army. The Qin army broke off and withdrew, and so he relieved Handan and preserved Zhao. The King of Zhao and Lord Pingyuan came in person to meet the Prince at the border, Lord Pingyuan carrying quiver and arrows on his back to lead the way for the Prince. The King of Zhao bowed twice and said: "Since antiquity no man of worth has come up to the Prince." At this time Lord Pingyuan dared not compare himself with others. When the Prince had parted from Master Hou and reached the army, Master Hou did indeed face north and cut his throat.

魏王怒公子之盗其兵符，矫杀晋鄙，公子亦自知也。已卻秦存赵，使将将其军归魏，而公子独与客留赵。赵孝成王德公子之矫夺晋鄙兵而存赵，乃与平原君计，以五城封公子。公子闻之，意骄矜而有自功之色。客有说公子曰：“物有不可忘，或有不可不忘。夫人有德於公子，公子不可忘也；公子有德於人，原公子忘之也。且矫魏王令，夺晋鄙兵以救赵，於赵则有功矣，於魏则未为忠臣也。公子乃自骄而功之，窃为公子不取也。”於是公子立自责，似若无所容者。赵王埽除自迎，执主人之礼，引公子就西阶。公子侧行辞让，从东阶上。自言罪过，以负於魏，无功於赵。赵王侍酒至暮，口不忍献五城，以公子退让也。公子竟留赵。赵王以鄗为公子汤沐邑，魏亦复以信陵奉公子。公子留赵。

The King of Wei was angry that the Prince had stolen his army tally and killed Jin Bi under a feigned command, and the Prince knew it himself. Having driven back Qin and preserved Zhao, he had a general lead his army back to Wei, while the Prince alone stayed in Zhao with his retainers. King Xiaocheng of Zhao, grateful to the Prince for seizing Jin Bi's army under a feigned command and preserving Zhao, planned with Lord Pingyuan to enfeoff the Prince with five Fortresses. When the Prince heard of it, his mind grew proud and he wore the look of one who takes credit for his deed. One of his retainers said to the Prince: "There are things that must not be forgotten, and things that must not be remembered. When another has done the Prince a kindness, the Prince must not forget it; when the Prince has done another a kindness, I would have the Prince forget it. Moreover, to feign the King of Wei's command and seize Jin Bi's army to relieve Zhao was a service to Zhao, but towards Wei it was not the act of a loyal subject. For the Prince to grow proud and take credit for it — I privately think the Prince should not." At this the Prince at once reproached himself, as if there were nowhere he could hide. The King of Zhao swept the way and came to meet him in person, performed the rites of a host, and led the Prince towards the western steps. The Prince stepped aside in refusal and went up by the eastern steps. He declared his own fault: he had failed Wei and done no service to Zhao. The King of Zhao kept him at wine until dusk, and could not bring himself to offer the five Fortresses, because of the Prince's self-abasement. In the end the Prince stayed in Zhao. The King of Zhao gave the Prince Hao as his private estate, and Wei too restored Xinling for the Prince's support. The Prince remained in Zhao.

公子闻赵有处士毛公藏於博徒，薛公藏於卖浆家，公子欲见两人，两人自匿不肯见公子。公子闻所在，乃间步往从此两人游，甚欢。平原君闻之，谓其夫人曰：“始吾闻夫人弟公子天下无双，今吾闻之，乃妄从博徒卖浆者游，公子妄人耳。”夫人以告公子。公子乃谢夫人去，曰：“始吾闻平原君贤，故负魏王而救赵，以称平原君。平原君之游，徒豪举耳，不求士也。无忌自在大梁时，常闻此两人贤，至赵，恐不得见。以无忌从之游，尚恐其不我欲也，今平原君乃以为羞，其不足从游。”乃装为去。夫人具以语平原君。平原君乃免冠谢，固留公子。平原君门下闻之，半去平原君归公子，天下士复往归公子，公子倾平原君客。

The Prince heard that in Zhao there were gentlemen in retirement: Master Mao, hidden among the gamblers, and Master Xue, hidden in the house of a seller of drinks. The Prince wished to see the two men, but the two hid themselves and would not see the Prince. When the Prince learned where they were, he went secretly on foot to keep company with the two, and they were very happy together. When Lord Pingyuan heard of it, he said to his wife: "At first I heard that your brother the Prince had no equal under Heaven; now I hear that he recklessly keeps company with gamblers and sellers of drinks — the Prince is a reckless man, that is all." His wife told the Prince. The Prince then took leave of his sister to go, saying: "At first I heard that Lord Pingyuan was a man of worth, and so I betrayed the King of Wei to relieve Zhao, to answer Lord Pingyuan's expectations. But Lord Pingyuan's company is only a grand display; he does not seek gentlemen. When Wuji was still in Daliang he often heard that these two were worthy men, and on coming to Zhao he feared he would not get to see them. With Wuji keeping their company, I still feared they would not want me; now Lord Pingyuan thinks it a disgrace — he is not worth keeping company with." And he packed to leave. His wife told Lord Pingyuan everything. Lord Pingyuan then took off his cap, apologised, and insisted that the Prince stay. When Lord Pingyuan's household heard of it, half of them left Lord Pingyuan and went over to the Prince, and the gentlemen of all under Heaven went over to the Prince once more; the Prince drew away Lord Pingyuan's retainers.

公子留赵十年不归。秦闻公子在赵，日夜出兵东伐魏。魏王患之，使使往请公子。公子恐其怒之，乃诫门下：“有敢为魏王使通者，死。”宾客皆背魏之赵，莫敢劝公子归。毛公、薛公两人往见公子曰：“公子所以重於赵，名闻诸侯者，徒以有魏也。今秦攻魏，魏急而公子不恤，使秦破大梁而夷先王之宗庙，公子当何面目立天下乎？”语未及卒，公子立变色，告车趣驾归救魏。

The Prince stayed in Zhao ten years without returning. Qin, hearing that the Prince was in Zhao, sent troops east day and night to attack Wei. The King of Wei was troubled by this and sent envoys to ask the Prince back. The Prince, fearing that the King was angry with him, warned his household: "Whoever dares to carry word for the King of Wei's envoys dies." His guests had all turned their backs on Wei for Zhao, and none dared urge the Prince to return. The two men Master Mao and Master Xue went to see the Prince and said: "The reason the Prince weighs heavily in Zhao and his name is heard among the Lords is solely that there is a Wei. Now Qin attacks Wei; Wei is in extremity and the Prince does not care. Should Qin destroy Daliang and level the ancestral temple of the former kings, with what face could the Prince stand before all under Heaven?" Before they had finished speaking the Prince's colour changed at once; he called for his carriage and hurried home to relieve Wei.

魏王见公子，相与泣，而以上将军印授公子，公子遂将。魏安釐王三十年，公子使使遍告诸侯。诸侯闻公子将，各遣将将兵救魏。公子率五国之兵破秦军於河外，走蒙骜。遂乘胜逐秦军至函谷关，抑秦兵，秦兵不敢出。当是时，公子威振天下，诸侯之客进兵法，公子皆名之，故世俗称魏公子兵法。

The King of Wei saw the Prince, and they wept together; and he gave the seal of Supreme General to the Prince, and the Prince took command. In the thirtieth year of King Anxi of Wei6 the Prince sent envoys to inform all the Lords. When the Lords heard that the Prince was in command, each sent generals with troops to relieve Wei. The Prince led the troops of five states and defeated the Qin army at Hewai, putting Meng Ao to flight. Then, following up the victory, he pursued the Qin army to Hangu Pass and held the Qin troops in check, so that the Qin troops dared not come out. At this time the Prince's majesty shook all under Heaven, and the retainers of the Lords presented methods of war, to all of which the Prince gave his name; hence the world at large calls them the Prince of Wei's Methods of War.

秦王患之，乃行金万斤於魏，求晋鄙客，令毁公子於魏王曰：“公子亡在外十年矣，今为魏将，诸侯将皆属，诸侯徒闻魏公子，不闻魏王。公子亦欲因此时定南面而王，诸侯畏公子之威，方欲共立之。”秦数使反间，伪贺公子得立为魏王未也。魏王日闻其毁，不能不信，後果使人代公子将。公子自知再以毁废，乃谢病不朝，与宾客为长夜饮，饮醇酒，多近妇女。日夜为乐饮者四岁，竟病酒而卒。其岁，魏安釐王亦薨。

The King of Qin was troubled by this, and sent ten thousand jin of gold into Wei to seek out Jin Bi's former retainers and have them slander the Prince to the King of Wei: "The Prince was ten years in exile abroad; now he is Wei's general, and the generals of the Lords are all under him; the Lords hear only of the Prince of Wei and not of the King of Wei. The Prince also wishes to seize this moment to face south and make himself king; the Lords, in awe of the Prince's majesty, are about to set him up together." Qin several times sent agents of discord, feigning to congratulate the Prince on whether he had yet been set up as King of Wei. The King of Wei heard the slander daily and could not help believing it, and afterwards did indeed send someone to replace the Prince in command. The Prince, knowing that he had a second time been cast aside through slander, pleaded illness and did not attend court; with his guests he drank the night through, drinking strong wine and keeping much company with women. Day and night he made merry with drink for four years, and in the end died of drink. That same year King Anxi of Wei also died.

秦闻公子死，使蒙骜攻魏，拔二十城，初置东郡。其後秦稍蚕食魏，十八岁而虏魏王，屠大梁。

When Qin heard that the Prince was dead, it sent Meng Ao to attack Wei; he took twenty Fortresses, and Dong Commandery was first established. Thereafter Qin ate away at Wei little by little, as a silkworm eats a leaf, and in eighteen years it captured the King of Wei and slaughtered Daliang.

高祖始微少时，数闻公子贤。及即天子位，每过大梁，常祠公子。高祖十二年，从击黥布还，为公子置守冢五家，世世岁以四时奉祠公子。

When Gaozu was still young and humble, he often heard of the Prince's worth. After he ascended the throne as Son of Heaven, whenever he passed Daliang he always sacrificed to the Prince. In the twelfth year of Gaozu, returning from the attack on Qing Bu, he established five tomb-keeping households for the Prince, to sacrifice to the Prince at the four seasons every year, generation after generation.

太史公曰：吾过大梁之墟，求问其所谓夷门。夷门者，城之东门也。天下诸公子亦有喜士者矣，然信陵君之接岩穴隐者，不耻下交，有以也。名冠诸侯，不虚耳。高祖每过之而令民奉祠不绝也。

The Grand Historian says: I passed the ruins of Daliang and sought out what is called the Yi Gate. The Yi Gate was the eastern gate of the Fortress. Among the princes of all under Heaven there have been others who delighted in gentlemen, but that Lord Xinling received recluses of the caves and cliffs, and was not ashamed to associate with those beneath him, had its reason. That his name stood first among the Lords was no empty thing. And Gaozu, whenever he passed, had the people sacrifice to him without cease.

范雎蔡泽列传：後数日，入朝，言於秦昭王曰：“客新有从山东来者曰蔡泽，其人辩士，明於三王之事，五伯之业，世俗之变，足以寄秦国之政。臣之见人甚众，莫及，臣不如也。臣敢以闻。”秦昭王召见，与语，大说之，拜为客卿。应侯因谢病请归相印。昭王彊起应侯，应侯遂称病笃。范雎免相，昭王新说蔡泽计画，遂拜为秦相，东收周室。

Biography of Fan Ju and Cai Ze: Some days later7 he went in to court and said to King Zhao of Qin: "Among the visitors there is one newly come from east of the mountains called Cai Ze, a man of eloquence, clear on the affairs of the Three Kings, the achievements of the Five Hegemons and the changes of the age — well able to be entrusted with the government of the state of Qin. Your servant has seen a great many men, and none comes up to him; your servant is not his equal. Your servant ventures to report it." King Zhao of Qin summoned him and spoke with him, was greatly pleased, and appointed him Guest Minister. Marquis Ying8 thereupon pleaded illness and asked to return the seal of Chancellor. King Zhao pressed Marquis Ying to rise, but Marquis Ying then declared himself gravely ill. Fan Ju was dismissed as Chancellor; King Zhao, newly pleased with Cai Ze's plans, then appointed him Chancellor of Qin, and eastward took in the house of Zhou.

蔡泽相秦数月，人或恶之，惧诛，乃谢病归相印，号为纲成君。居秦十馀年，事昭王、孝文王、庄襄王。卒事始皇帝，为秦使於燕，三年而燕使太子丹入质於秦。

Cai Ze had been Chancellor of Qin for some months when someone spoke ill of him; fearing execution, he pleaded illness and returned the seal of Chancellor, and was styled Lord Gangcheng. He lived in Qin more than ten years, serving King Zhao, King Xiaowen and King Zhuangxiang. Last he served the First Emperor, and went as Qin's envoy to Yan; after three years Yan sent Heir Apparent Dan as a hostage to Qin.

廉颇蔺相如列传：
明年，秦兵遂围邯郸，岁馀，几不得脱。赖楚、魏诸侯来救，乃得解邯郸之围。赵王亦以括母先言，竟不诛也。

Biography of Lian Po and Lin Xiangru:
The next year9 the Qin troops went on to besiege Handan, and for more than a year it barely escaped. Thanks to the Lords of Chu and Wei coming to its relief, the siege of Handan was lifted. The King of Zhao, too, because Kuo's mother had spoken beforehand, in the end did not put her to death.

Terms

Prince / 公子n. a son of a ruling king or lord.
Chancellor / 相n. the chief minister of a state.
gentlemen / 士n. men of the gentleman class, from whom officers and retainers were drawn.
retainers / 食客; 宾客n. guests kept at a lord's table; the clients of a great house.
the Lords / 诸侯npl. the rulers of the states, collectively; in this period the kings of the warring states and the lesser lords, as distinct from the Zhou king.
recluse / 隐士n. a gentleman who withholds himself from office.
General / 将军n. commander of an army; used as a title before the name.
tally; tiger tally / 兵符; 虎符n. the split tally, often in the form of a tiger, of which ruler and general each held a half; a command was valid only when the halves were matched.
the Five Hegemons / 五霸; 五伯n. the five lords who in turn led the states in the Spring and Autumn period.
Fortress / 城n. a walled place.
private estate / 汤沐邑n. literally a bath-and-ablution town: a town whose revenue was assigned to a person for private expenses.
gentlemen in retirement / 处士n. gentlemen of worth living in private, without office.
Supreme General / 上将军n. the commander-in-chief.
agents of discord / 反间n. agents sent to sow division at an enemy court.
Son of Heaven / 天子n. the emperor.
tomb-keeping households / 守冢n. households assigned to tend a tomb and keep up its sacrifices.
the Grand Historian / 太史公n. Sima Qian's own title, introducing his appraisal at the end of a chapter.
east of the mountains / 山东n. the lands east of the Xiao mountains: the states other than Qin.
the Three Kings / 三王n. the founders of the Xia, Shang and Zhou.
Guest Minister / 客卿n. a minister of foreign origin at the Qin court.
hostage / 质n. a hostage sent to another court as surety.
