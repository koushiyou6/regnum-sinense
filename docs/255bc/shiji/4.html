Introduction

[§4] Shiji IV: Biographies

春申君列传：春申君者，楚人也，名歇，姓黄氏。游学博闻，事楚顷襄王。顷襄王以歇为辩，使於秦。秦昭王使白起攻韩、魏，败之於华阳，禽魏将芒卯，韩、魏服而事秦。秦昭王方令白起与韩、魏共伐楚，未行，而楚使黄歇適至於秦，闻秦之计。当是之时，秦已前使白起攻楚，取巫、黔中之郡，拔鄢郢，东至竟陵，楚顷襄王东徙治於陈县。黄歇见楚怀王之为秦所诱而入朝，遂见欺，留死於秦。顷襄王，其子也，秦轻之，恐壹举兵而灭楚。歇乃上书说秦昭王曰：

Biography of Lord Chunshen: Lord Chunshen was a man of Chu, named Xie, of the clan Huang. He travelled to study and was widely learned, and served King Qingxiang of Chu. King Qingxiang, holding Xie to be eloquent, sent him as envoy to Qin. King Zhao of Qin had sent Bai Qi to attack Han and Wei; he defeated them at Huayang and captured the Wei general Mang Mao, and Han and Wei submitted and served Qin. King Zhao of Qin was just then ordering Bai Qi to join Han and Wei in an attack on Chu, and before he had set out the Chu envoy Huang Xie happened to arrive in Qin and learned of Qin's plan. At that time Qin had already, earlier, sent Bai Qi to attack Chu; he had taken the Commanderies of Wu and Qianzhong, taken Yan and Ying, and reached Jingling in the east, and King Qingxiang of Chu had moved his seat east to Chen County. Huang Xie had seen how King Huai of Chu, lured by Qin into coming to court, had been deceived and kept in Qin until he died. King Qingxiang was his son; Qin thought little of him, and Xie feared that it would raise its troops in a single stroke and extinguish Chu. Xie therefore sent up a letter to persuade King Zhao of Qin:

天下莫彊於秦、楚。今闻大王欲伐楚，此犹两虎相与斗。两虎相与斗而驽犬受其弊，不如善楚。臣请言其说：臣闻物至则反，冬夏是也；致至则危，累釭是也。今大国之地，遍天下有其二垂，此从生民已来，万乘之地未尝有也。先帝文王、庄王之身，三世不妄接地於齐，以绝从亲之要。今王使盛桥守事於韩，盛桥以其地入秦，是王不用甲，不信威，而得百里之地。王可谓能矣。王又举甲而攻魏，杜大梁之门，举河内，拔燕、酸枣、虚、桃，入邢，魏之兵云翔而不敢捄。王之功亦多矣。王休甲息众，二年而後复之；又并蒲、衍、首、垣，以临仁、平丘，黄、济阳婴城而魏氏服；王又割濮〔磿〕之北，注齐秦之要，绝楚赵之脊，天下五合六聚而不敢救。王之威亦单矣。

"Under Heaven none is stronger than Qin and Chu. Now I hear that the great King wishes to attack Chu. This is like two tigers fighting each other: when two tigers fight, the worthless dog profits from their exhaustion. Better to be on good terms with Chu. Let your servant set out his argument. Your servant has heard that when a thing reaches its limit it turns back, as winter and summer do; and that what is heaped to the limit is in danger, as stacked pieces1 are. Now the great state's territory reaches both ends of all under Heaven; since there have been people, no land of ten thousand chariots has ever been so. In the persons of the former kings, King Wen and King Zhuang, for three generations you never rashly extended your lands to touch Qi, so as to cut the waist of the Vertical league. Now the King has Sheng Qiao hold office in Han, and Sheng Qiao brings its land into Qin: thus without using armour or displaying might the King has gained a hundred li of land. The King may be called able. The King also raised armour and attacked Wei, shut the gates of Daliang, took Henei, took Yan, Suanzao, Xu and Tao, and entered Xing, and Wei's troops hovered like clouds and dared not come to the rescue. The King's achievements are many indeed. The King rested his armour and gave his people respite, and after two years took it up again; he also annexed Pu, Yan, Shou and Yuan, and bore down on Ren and Pingqiu; Huang and Jiyang shut their Fortresses, and the house of Wei submitted. The King also cut off the land north of Puli2, pinned the waist between Qi and Qin, and severed the spine between Chu and Zhao; all under Heaven joined five times and gathered six, and dared not come to the rescue. The King's might has been exerted to the full3.

王若能持功守威，绌攻取之心而肥仁义之地，使无後患，三王不足四，五伯不足六也。王若负人徒之众，仗兵革之彊，乘毁魏之威，而欲以力臣天下之主，臣恐其有後患也。诗曰“靡不有初，鲜克有终”。易曰“狐涉水，濡其尾”。此言始之易，终之难也。何以知其然也？昔智氏见伐赵之利而不知榆次之祸，吴见伐齐之便而不知干隧之败。此二国者，非无大功也，没利於前而易患於後也。吴之信越也，从而伐齐，既胜齐人於艾陵，还为越王禽三渚之浦。智氏之信韩、魏也，从而伐赵，攻晋阳城，胜有日矣，韩、魏叛之，杀智伯瑶於凿台之下。今王妒楚之不毁也，而忘毁楚之彊韩、魏也，臣为王虑而不取也。

"If the King can hold on to his achievements and keep his majesty, curb the urge to attack and seize and fatten the ground of humanity and right, so that there is no trouble to come, then the Three Kings will no longer lack a fourth, nor the Five Hegemons a sixth. If instead the King relies on the multitude of his people and the strength of his arms, and rides the prestige of having broken Wei, to subject the rulers of all under Heaven by force, your servant fears there will be trouble to come. The Odes say: 'None but has a beginning; few can have an end.' The Changes say: 'The fox crosses the water and wets its tail.' This says that beginnings are easy and endings hard. How do we know it is so? Of old the Zhi saw the profit of attacking Zhao and did not foresee the calamity of Yuci; Wu saw the convenience of attacking Qi and did not foresee the defeat at Gansui. These two states did not lack great achievements; they sank into the profit before them and made light of the trouble behind. Wu, trusting Yue, went on to attack Qi, and having beaten the men of Qi at Ailing, on its return was captured by the King of Yue at the shore of Sanzhu. The Zhi, trusting Han and Wei, went on to attack Zhao and assaulted the Fortress of Jinyang; victory was only days away when Han and Wei turned on them and killed Zhi Bo Yao beneath the Zao Terrace. Now the King begrudges that Chu is not destroyed, and forgets that destroying Chu would strengthen Han and Wei. Your servant, planning for the King, would not have it.

诗曰“大武远宅而不涉”。从此观之，楚国，援也；邻国，敌也。诗云“趯趯〔毚〕免，还犬获之。他人有心，余忖度之”。今王中道而信韩、魏之善王也，此正吴之信越也。臣闻之，敌不可假，时不可失。臣恐韩、魏卑辞除患而实欲欺大国也。何则？王无重世之德於韩、魏，而有累世之怨焉。夫韩、魏父子兄弟接踵而死於秦者将十世矣。本国残，社稷坏，宗庙毁。刳腹绝肠，折颈摺颐，首身分离，暴骸骨於草泽，头颅僵仆，相望於境，父子老弱系脰束手为群虏者相及於路。鬼神孤伤，无所血食。人民不聊生，族类离散，流亡为仆妾者，盈满海内矣。故韩、魏之不亡，秦社稷之忧也，今王资之与攻楚，不亦过乎！

"The Odes say: 'Great arms do not go far afield to trespass.4' Seen in this light, the state of Chu is a support, and the neighbouring states are enemies. The Odes say: 'Hopping, hopping the wily hare; it meets a dog and is caught. What is in another's mind, I can fathom.5' Now the King midway trusts that Han and Wei mean him well; this is exactly Wu's trusting of Yue. Your servant has heard that an enemy must not be indulged, and a moment must not be lost. Your servant fears that Han and Wei speak humbly to be rid of their peril but in truth mean to deceive the great state. Why? The King has no kindness of generations' standing toward Han and Wei, but a grudge heaped up over generations. Fathers and sons, elder and younger brothers of Han and Wei have died at Qin's hands heel upon heel for nearly ten generations. Their homelands are ravaged, their altars of soil and grain overthrown, their ancestral temples destroyed. Bellies ripped open and bowels cut out, necks broken and jaws wrenched, heads parted from bodies, bones exposed in the marshes, skulls lying stiff within sight of one another across the borders; fathers and sons, old and young, roped by the neck and bound at the hands, driven in herds of captives, follow one another along the roads. Their ghosts and spirits are forlorn and wounded, with none to offer them blood sacrifice. The people cannot live, clans are scattered, and those who have fled to become servants and concubines fill all within the seas. So that Han and Wei have not perished is the worry of Qin's altars of soil and grain; and now the King would furnish them with means and attack Chu with them — is that not a mistake!

且王攻楚将恶出兵？王将借路於仇雠之韩、魏乎？兵出之日而王忧其不返也，是王以兵资於仇雠之韩、魏也。王若不借路於仇雠之韩、魏，必攻随水右壤。随水右壤，此皆广川大水，山林谿谷，不食之地也，王虽有之，不为得地。是王有毁楚之名而无得地之实也。

"Moreover, when the King attacks Chu, by what road will he send out his troops? Will the King borrow a road through Han and Wei, his sworn enemies? From the day the troops set out the King will worry that they will not return; that is the King lending his troops to his enemies Han and Wei. If the King does not borrow a road through his enemies Han and Wei, he must attack the lands on the right bank of Sui River. The right bank of Sui River is all broad rivers and great waters, mountain forests and ravines, land that yields no food; though the King should hold it, it would not count as gaining land. Thus the King would have the name of destroying Chu without the substance of gaining land.

且王攻楚之日，四国必悉起兵以应王。秦、楚之兵构而不离，魏氏将出而攻留、方与、铚、湖陵、砀、萧、相，故宋必尽。齐人南面攻楚，泗上必举。此皆平原四达，膏腴之地，而使独攻。王破楚以肥韩、魏於中国而劲齐。韩、魏之彊，足以校於秦。齐南以泗水为境，东负海，北倚河，而无後患，天下之国莫彊於齐、魏，齐、魏得地葆利而详事下吏，一年之後，为帝未能，其於禁王之为帝有馀矣。

"Moreover, on the day the King attacks Chu, the four states will surely raise all their troops to answer the King. While the troops of Qin and Chu are locked together and cannot disengage, the house of Wei will march out and attack Liu, Fangyu, Zhi, Huling, Dang, Xiao and Xiang, and the old land of Song will be theirs entirely. The men of Qi, facing south, will attack Chu, and the Si country will surely be taken. These are all open plains with roads on four sides, rich and fertile land, and they would be left to attack it unopposed. The King would break Chu only to fatten Han and Wei in the central states and to strengthen Qi. The strength of Han and Wei would then suffice to contend with Qin. Qi, with Si River for its border on the south, the sea at its back on the east and the River to lean on in the north, would have nothing to fear behind it; of the states under Heaven none would be stronger than Qi and Wei. Qi and Wei, having gained land and secured its profit, and attending carefully to their lesser officers, in a year's time might not yet be able to become Emperor, but would be more than able to prevent the King from becoming Emperor.

夫以王壤土之博，人徒之众，兵革之彊，壹举事而树怨於楚，迟令韩、魏归帝重於齐，是王失计也。臣为王虑，莫若善楚。秦、楚合而为一以临韩，韩必敛手。王施以东山之险，带以曲河之利，韩必为关内之侯。若是而王以十万戍郑，梁氏寒心，许、鄢陵婴城，而上蔡、召陵不往来也，如此而魏亦关内侯矣。王壹善楚，而关内两万乘之主注地於齐，齐右壤可拱手而取也。王之地一经两海，要约天下，是燕、赵无齐、楚，齐、楚无燕、赵也。然後危动燕、赵，直摇齐、楚，此四国者不待痛而服矣。

"With the breadth of the King's territory, the multitude of his people and the strength of his arms, to raise one campaign and plant a grudge in Chu, and so drive Han and Wei to carry the weight of the imperial title over to Qi — that is the King miscalculating. If your servant plans for the King, nothing is better than to be on good terms with Chu. If Qin and Chu unite as one and bear down on Han, Han must fold its hands. If the King spreads out the defences of the eastern mountains and girds himself with the advantage of the bend of the River, Han must become a Marquis within the Passes. If then the King garrisons Zheng with a hundred thousand, the house of Liang will turn cold at heart; Xu and Yanling will shut their Fortresses, and Shangcai and Shaoling will no longer come and go; and so Wei too will be a Marquis within the Passes. Once the King is on good terms with Chu, two lords of ten thousand chariots within the Passes will turn their arms upon Qi6, and Qi's western lands7 may be taken with folded hands. The King's land would run in one stretch from sea to sea, binding all under Heaven: then Yan and Zhao would be without Qi and Chu, and Qi and Chu without Yan and Zhao. After that, threaten and shake Yan and Zhao, and rock Qi and Chu outright, and these four states will submit without waiting to be hurt."

昭王曰：“善。”於是乃止白起而谢韩、魏。发使赂楚，约为与国。

King Zhao said: "Good." Thereupon he halted Bai Qi and sent his excuses to Han and Wei. He sent envoys with gifts to Chu and bound it as an allied state.

黄歇受约归楚，楚使歇与太子完入质於秦，秦留之数年。楚顷襄王病，太子不得归。而楚太子与秦相应侯善，於是黄歇乃说应侯曰：“相国诚善楚太子乎？”应侯曰：“然。”歇曰：“今楚王恐不起疾，秦不如归其太子。太子得立，其事秦必重而德相国无穷，是亲与国而得储万乘也。若不归，则咸阳一布衣耳；楚更立太子，必不事秦。夫失与国而绝万乘之和，非计也。原相国孰虑之。”应侯以闻秦王。秦王曰：“令楚太子之傅先往问楚王之疾，返而後图之。”黄歇为楚太子计曰：“秦之留太子也，欲以求利也。今太子力未能有以利秦也，歇忧之甚。而阳文君子二人在中，王若卒大命，太子不在，阳文君子必立为後，太子不得奉宗庙矣。不如亡秦，与使者俱出；臣请止，以死当之。”楚太子因变衣服为楚使者御以出关，而黄歇守舍，常为谢病。度太子已远，秦不能追，歇乃自言秦昭王曰：“楚太子已归，出远矣。歇当死，原赐死。”昭王大怒，欲听其自杀也。应侯曰：“歇为人臣，出身以徇其主，太子立，必用歇，故不如无罪而归之，以亲楚。”秦因遣黄歇。

Huang Xie received the covenant and returned to Chu, and Chu sent Xie with the Heir Apparent, Wan, as hostages to Qin; Qin kept them for several years. King Qingxiang of Chu fell ill, and the Heir Apparent could not return. Now the Heir Apparent of Chu was on good terms with the Chancellor of Qin, Marquis Ying, and so Huang Xie spoke to Marquis Ying: "Is the Chancellor of State truly a friend to the Heir Apparent of Chu?" Marquis Ying said: "I am." Xie said: "Now the King of Chu is unlikely to rise from his sickness, and Qin would do best to send his Heir Apparent home. If the Heir Apparent is set on the throne, his service to Qin will surely be weighty and his gratitude to the Chancellor of State without end; that is to befriend an allied state and lay up a ruler of ten thousand chariots. If he is not sent home, he is merely a commoner of Xianyang; Chu will set up another Heir Apparent, who will surely not serve Qin. To lose an allied state and break off the friendship of a ruler of ten thousand chariots is no plan. I wish the Chancellor of State to consider it carefully." Marquis Ying reported it to the King of Qin. The King of Qin said: "Let the Heir Apparent of Chu's Tutor go first to inquire after the King of Chu's illness; when he returns we shall plan it." Huang Xie planned for the Heir Apparent of Chu: "Qin keeps the Heir Apparent because it wants to gain some advantage by it. Now the Heir Apparent has no means yet to benefit Qin, and Xie is deeply anxious about it. And Lord Yangwen's two sons are at court; if the King should die and the Heir Apparent is not there, Lord Yangwen's son will surely be set up as successor, and the Heir Apparent will never serve the ancestral temple. Better to flee Qin and go out with the envoy; your servant begs to stay behind and answer for it with his life." The Heir Apparent of Chu accordingly changed his clothes and, as the Chu envoy's driver, went out through the Pass, while Huang Xie kept the lodging and constantly made excuses of illness. Judging that the Heir Apparent was already far away and Qin could not overtake him, Xie then spoke for himself to King Zhao of Qin: "The Heir Apparent of Chu has gone home; he is already far away. Xie deserves death; I beg to be granted death." King Zhao was greatly angered and was ready to let him kill himself. Marquis Ying said: "Xie, as a subject, gave his own life for his master's sake; when the Heir Apparent is set up he will surely employ Xie. So it is better to send him home without blame, to befriend Chu." Qin accordingly sent Huang Xie back.

歇至楚三月，楚顷襄王卒，太子完立，是为考烈王。考烈王元年，以黄歇为相，封为春申君，赐淮北地十二县。後十五岁，黄歇言之楚王曰：“淮北地边齐，其事急，请以为郡便。”因并献淮北十二县。请封於江东。考烈王许之。春申君因城故吴墟，以自为都邑。

Xie had been in Chu three months when King Qingxiang of Chu died and the Heir Apparent, Wan, was set up: this was King Kaolie. In the first year of King Kaolie8 he made Huang Xie Chancellor, enfeoffed him as Lord Chunshen, and granted him twelve Counties of the land north of the Huai. Fifteen years later Huang Xie said to the King of Chu: "The land north of the Huai borders Qi, and its affairs are pressing; it would be better made a Commandery." And he gave back the twelve Counties north of the Huai, asking to be enfeoffed in Jiangdong. King Kaolie consented. Lord Chunshen accordingly fortified the old ruins of Wu and made them his seat.

春申君既相楚，是时齐有孟尝君，赵有平原君，魏有信陵君，方争下士，招致宾客，以相倾夺，辅国持权。

When Lord Chunshen had become Chancellor of Chu, at that time Qi had Lord Mengchang, Zhao had Lord Pingyuan and Wei had Lord Xinling, all vying to humble themselves before gentlemen and to attract retainers, so as to outdo one another, support their states and hold power.

春申君为楚相四年，秦破赵之长平军四十馀万。五年，围邯郸。邯郸告急於楚，楚使春申君将兵往救之，秦兵亦去，春申君归。春申君相楚八年，为楚北伐灭鲁，以荀卿为兰陵令。当是时，楚复彊。

In the fourth year of Lord Chunshen's chancellorship of Chu, Qin destroyed Zhao's army of more than four hundred thousand at Changping. In the fifth year it besieged Handan. Handan sent word of its peril to Chu; Chu sent Lord Chunshen with troops to relieve it, the Qin troops also withdrew, and Lord Chunshen returned. In the eighth year9 of his chancellorship Lord Chunshen campaigned north for Chu and extinguished Lu, and made Xun Qing Sheriff of Lanling. At this time Chu was strong again.

赵平原君使人於春申君，春申君舍之於上舍。赵使欲夸楚，为玳瑁簪，刀剑室以珠玉饰之，请命春申君客。春申君客三千馀人，其上客皆蹑珠履以见赵使，赵使大惭。

Lord Pingyuan of Zhao sent a man to Lord Chunshen, and Lord Chunshen lodged him in the best quarters. The Zhao envoy wished to show off before Chu: he had tortoiseshell hairpins made and sword scabbards adorned with pearls and jade, and asked to meet Lord Chunshen's retainers. Lord Chunshen's retainers numbered more than three thousand, and his chief guests all came to see the Zhao envoy shod in pearl-studded slippers; the Zhao envoy was greatly ashamed.

Terms

Commandery / 郡n. the largest territorial unit of a state, above the County.
ten thousand chariots / 万乘n. the measure of a great state's strength; a ruler of ten thousand chariots is a king.
the Vertical Alliance / 从; 纵n. the north–south league of the eastern states against Qin.
Fortress / 城n. a walled place.
the Three Kings / 三王n. the founders of the Xia, Shang and Zhou.
the Five Hegemons / 五霸; 五伯n. the five lords who in turn led the states in the Spring and Autumn period.
altars of soil and grain / 社稷n. the altars of a state's gods of soil and grain, standing for the state itself.
ancestral temple / 宗庙n. the temple of a ruling house's ancestors.
within the seas / 海内n. the whole inhabited world.
Emperor / 帝n. the title above king, claimed by Qin and Qi in these years.
Marquis within the Passes / 关内侯; 关内之侯n. a lord whose land lies inside Qin's passes: a dependant of Qin.
allied state / 与国n. a state bound by treaty.
Heir Apparent / 太子n. the designated successor of a king.
hostage / 质n. a hostage sent to another court as surety.
Chancellor / 相n. the chief minister of a state.
Chancellor of State / 相国n. the chief minister, addressed by his full title.
commoner / 布衣n. literally one in hempen clothes: a man without rank.
Tutor / 傅n. the officer in charge of an heir's upbringing.
Pass / 关n. a fortified pass; here the Hangu Pass, Qin's eastern gate.
County / 县n. the basic unit of direct administration, capitalized as an administrative unit.
gentlemen / 士n. men of the gentleman class, from whom officers and retainers were drawn.
retainers / 食客; 宾客n. guests kept at a lord's table; the clients of a great house.
Sheriff / 令n. the officer in charge of a County.

Notes

1 stacked pieces累釭: so the text; the three-commentary edition reads 累棊, "stacked gaming-pieces", which is translated.
2 Puli濮磿: 磿 is supplied from the three-commentary edition. Xu Guang: the Pu flows north into the Ji at Juye; the Suoyin: a place-name, near the Pu.
3 exerted to the full单: read 殚 (Xu Guang); the Suoyin: "exhausted — the King's might has been fully exerted."
4 Great arms大武远宅而不涉: rendered with the Zhengyi, "a great army does not go far afield on campaign."
5 the wily hare趯趯毚兔，还犬获之: 毚 is supplied from the three-commentary edition, which also reads 还; 免 is for 兔.
6 turn their arms upon Qi注地於齐: the Suoyin, "注 means to cut at it with troops."
7 western lands右壤: the Zhengyi, "the lands north and south of Jizhou", that is, Qi's western border on the Ji.
8 first year of King Kaolie262 BC.
9 eighth yearThe eighth year of King Kaolie, 255 BC. The Suoyin: the Table has the taking of Lu and the enfeoffment of its lord at Ju in this year, and Lu's extinction in the fourteenth.
