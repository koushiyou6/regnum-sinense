Shiji I: Basic Annals

Shiji 史记 · 246 BC · 秦王政元年

Introduction

The Basic Annals of the First Emperor at his accession: the extent of Qin at the start of the reign, Lü Buwei as Chancellor and Marquis Wenxin with Li Si in his household, the generals Meng Ao, Wang Yi and Lord Biao, and in the first year the revolt of Jinyang and Meng Ao's settling of it.

Annotations

[§1] 史记（一：本纪）

[§1] Shiji I: Basic Annals

秦始皇本纪：年十三岁，庄襄王死，政代立为秦王。当是之时，秦地已并巴、蜀、汉中，越宛有郢，置南郡矣；北收上郡以东，有河东、太原、上党郡；东至荥阳，灭二周，置三川郡。吕不韦为相，封十万户，号曰文信侯。招致宾客游士，欲以并天下。李斯为舍人。蒙骜、王齮、麃公等为将军。王年少，初即位，委国事大臣。

Basic Annals of the First Emperor of Qin: When he was thirteen1, King Zhuangxiang died, and Zheng took his place as King of Qin. At this time Qin's territory had already annexed Ba, Shu and Hanzhong, and, passing beyond Wan, held Ying, where Nan Commandery had been set up; in the north it had taken in Shang Commandery and the lands east of it, and held the Commanderies of Hedong, Taiyuan and Shangdang; in the east it reached Xingyang, having wiped out the two Zhou2 and set up Sanchuan Commandery. Lü Buwei was Chancellor, enfeoffed with a hundred thousand households and styled Marquis Wenxin. He drew retainers and travelling gentlemen to him, meaning by them to annex all under Heaven. Li Si was a household retainer3. Meng Ao, Wang Yi4, Lord Biao5 and others were Generals. The king was young and newly on the throne, and left the affairs of state to the great ministers.

晋阳反，元年，将军蒙骜击定之。

Jinyang revolted; in the first year6 the General Meng Ao attacked and settled it.

Terms

Commandery / 郡n. the largest territorial unit of a state, above the County.
Chancellor / 相n. the chief minister of a state.
household / 户n. the household, the unit by which a fief's population and revenue were reckoned.
retainers / 食客; 宾客n. guests kept at a lord's table; the clients of a great house.
household retainer / 舍人n. a retainer attached to a lord's household.
General / 将军n. commander of an army; used as a title before the name.

Persons

King Zhuangxiang of Qin 秦庄襄王King of Qin, reigned 249–247 BC; personal name Zichu 子楚 (Suoyin).
the First Emperor of Qin 秦始皇帝Ying Zheng 嬴政, King of Qin from his accession in 247 BC, his first year 246 BC; First Emperor from 221 BC.
Lü Buwei 吕不韦Chancellor of State of Qin, Marquis Wenxin 文信侯, styled Second Father 仲父 by King Zheng; by origin a merchant of Yangdi.
Li Si 李斯A man of Shangcai in Chu, pupil of Xun Qing; came to Qin in 247 BC and entered Lü Buwei's household as a Court Gentleman; later Senior Clerk, Guest Minister, Commandant of Justice and Chancellor of Qin.
Meng Ao 蒙骜General of Qin; a man of Qi, father of Meng Wu and grandfather of Meng Tian (Suoyin).
Wang Yi 王齮General of Qin; the Suoyin identifies him with Wang He 王龁, who in King Zhaoxiang's forty-ninth year replaced the Grand Master Ling in the attack on Zhao.
Lord Biao 麃公A general of Qin under King Zheng; the Suoyin: the lord of the town of Biao, his surname and name lost; the Zhengyi: Biao is probably a County town of Qin, and a Grand Master is called 公 as in the Chu system.

Places

Qin 秦the state of Qin, with its capital at Xianyang, Shaanxi.
Ba 巴the state and region of Ba on the upper Yangtze, taken by Qin in 316 BC; modern Chongqing.
Shu 蜀the state and region of Shu, the Chengdu plain, taken by Qin in 316 BC; the 西蜀 of the memorial.
Hanzhong 汉中the upper Han valley, taken from Chu by Qin; the Zhengyi: six hundred li, in King Huiwen's thirteenth year; modern Hanzhong, Shaanxi.
Wan 宛a city of Chu on the upper Han; modern Nanyang 南阳, Henan.
Ying 郢the capital of Chu; the Jinan city site north of modern Jingzhou, Hubei.
Nan Commandery 南郡Qin's Commandery on the old Chu capital region about Ying, set up in 278 BC; modern Jingzhou, Hubei.
Shang Commandery 上郡the Commandery north of the Wei on the Luo, ceded by Wei to Qin; the Zhengyi: fifteen Counties, in King Huiwen's tenth year; modern northern Shaanxi.
Hedong Commandery 河东郡Qin's Commandery east of the Yellow River's southward course, the old Wei lands of south-western Shanxi; modern Yuncheng.
Taiyuan 太原the basin of the middle Fen River, Zhao's north-western region, made a Commandery of Qin; modern Taiyuan, Shanxi.
Shangdang 上党the upland region between the Taihang and the Fen, held in parts by Han and Zhao and contested with Qin; modern Changzhi 长治, Shanxi.
Xingyang 荥阳a Han town east of Chenggao; modern Xingyang, Henan.
Sanchuan Commandery 三川郡a Qin Commandery, named for its three rivers, the Yellow River, Luo River and Yi River (Wei Zhao); established in 249 BC on the lands of Zhou and Han around Luoyang; renamed Henan Commandery under the Han (Pei Yin).
Jinyang 晋阳the Zhao stronghold; modern Taiyuan, Shanxi.

Notes

1 When he was thirteen年十三岁: King Zhuangxiang died in 247 BC; Zheng's first year as king is this year, 246 BC.
2 the two Zhou二周: the Western and Eastern Zhou domains, extinguished in 256 and 249 BC (see those years).
3 household retainer舍人: the Jijie cites Wen Ying: the title of a petty officer in charge of the stables; or, retainers in attendance are called sheren.
4 Wang Yi王齮: the Jijie cites Xu Guang, one text 齕. The Suoyin: Meng Ao was a man of Qi, father of Meng Wu and grandfather of Meng Tian; Wang Yi is Wang He 王齕, who in King Zhao's forty-ninth year replaced the Grand Master Ling in attacking Zhao. The Zhengyi gives the reading of 齮 as yi (魚綺反); Liu Bozhuang reads it qi.
5 Lord Biao麃公: the Jijie cites Ying Shao, Biao is a Qin town; the Suoyin: probably the lord of the town of Biao, his surname and name lost to history; the Zhengyi: 麃 read biao (彼苗反), probably a County town of Qin, and a Grand Master is called 公 as in the Chu system.
6 in the first year246 BC. The Table (§2) and the Hereditary House of Zhao (§3) record the taking of Jinyang.
