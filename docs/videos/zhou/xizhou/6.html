约公元前950年—公元前900年

c. 950–900 BC

History of the Zhou Dynasty · Western Zhou · 西周

Introduction

The later reign of King Mu and the reign of King Gong, about 950 to 900 BC: King Mu's wars with the Huaiyi and the eastern states in the Ban gui, and Dong's fight at Hu; Yu's campaigns against the Guifang in the Yu ding; the little that is recorded of war under King Gong; and the accession of King Yi (懿).

Annotations

[§6] 约公元前950年—公元前900年

[§6] c. 950–900 BC

穆王晚期伐淮夷与东国，考古发现可为证据；《班簋》其铭云：“三年静东国，亡不成。”另有铭文指出，此时淮夷侵伐内地，穆王命出征并与其战于胡国。

In his later years King Mu attacked the Huaiyi and the eastern states, as archaeological finds bear witness; the inscription of the Ban gui1 says: "In three years he brought peace to the eastern states, and none did not submit." Another inscription shows that at this time the Huaiyi invaded the interior, and King Mu ordered Dong to take the field, who fought them at the state of Hu.

据出土的大小盂鼎，穆王时亦有盂奉王命两次征伐鬼方的记载；伐鬼方第一次就大有斩获，捉得鬼方的首领三人，割耳四千八百多人，俘一万三千多人，车三十辆，牛三百多头，并回归举行了献俘礼。

According to the excavated Da Yu ding and Xiao Yu ding, in King Mu's time there are also records of Yu twice campaigning against the Guifang at the King's command; the very first campaign against the Guifang brought great gains: three chiefs of the Guifang were captured, the ears of more than four thousand eight hundred men were cut off, more than thirteen thousand men were taken prisoner, with thirty chariots and more than three hundred oxen, and on his return the rite of presenting the captives was held.

穆王去世后，其子繄扈立，即为共王；就目前所知，属于共王世记有战争的器铭者，尚未发现，有一些记有战争的器铭由于不能完全断定究竟属于共、懿还是孝王，只好暂归于“西周中期”。

After King Mu's death his son Yihu was set on the throne; he is King Gong. So far as is known at present, no vessel inscription recording war that belongs to King Gong's reign has yet been found; some vessel inscriptions that record war cannot be assigned with certainty to King Gong, King Yi (懿) or King Xiao, and can only be placed for the time being under the "middle Western Zhou".

关于共王时代的征战，传世文献所载的仅有司马迁的《史记·周本纪》中共王灭密一事。

As to the wars of King Gong's time, all that the transmitted texts record is King Gong's destruction of Mi2, in Sima Qian's Shiji, Basic Annals of Zhou.

共王去世后，其子囏继位，即为懿王。

After King Gong's death his son Jian succeeded; he is King Yi (懿).

Terms

the Huaiyi / 淮夷n. the Yi peoples of the Huai region.
Ban gui / 班簋n. a bronze gui of the reign of King Mu, cast by Ban, whose inscription records three years of campaigning in the eastern states.
Da Yu ding and Xiao Yu ding / 大小盂鼎n. two bronze ding cast by Yu; the Xiao Yu ding records his campaigns against the Guifang and the presentation of the captives.
the Guifang / 鬼方n. a people of the north-west in the oracle-bone inscriptions and the Zhouyi, attacked by Gaozong (Wu Ding).

Persons

King Mu of Zhou 周穆王King of Zhou, son and successor of King Zhao; personal name Man 满.
Dong A commander of King Mu, who fought the Huaiyi at Hu; the maker of the Dong gui. The name is also read Zhong.
Yu 盂The maker of the Da Yu ding and the Xiao Yu ding, who twice campaigned against the Guifang at the King's command.
King Gong of Zhou 周共王King of Zhou, son and successor of King Mu; personal name Yihu 繄扈.
Sima Qian 司马迁The author of the Shiji, Grand Historian under Emperor Wu of Han.
King Yi of Zhou 周懿王King of Zhou, son and successor of King Gong; personal name Jian 囏.

Places

Hu 胡a state of the Ying and Huai country where Dong fought the Huaiyi; traditionally placed at Yancheng, Luohe, Henan, or at Fuyang, Anhui.
Mixu 密须a state attacked and destroyed by King Wen; traditionally placed at Lingtai, Pingliang, Gansu.

Notes

1 Ban gui班簋: translated after Robert Eno (Inscriptional Records of the Western Zhou, no. 46), who has "bring peace to the eastern lands" and "none do not submit".
2 Mi密: the state of Mi, traditionally placed at Lingtai, Gansu, on the land of the former Mixu.
