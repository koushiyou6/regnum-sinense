约公元前1260年—公元前1235年

c. 1260–1235 BC

History of the Zhou Dynasty · Shang · 商

Introduction

Wu Ding's wars as the oracle-bone inscriptions and archaeology show them, from about 1260 to 1235 BC: the conquest of southern Shanxi, Zi Hua's fief in the east, the campaigns of Que, Zhi Guo, Fu Zi and Fu Geng in the west, the defeat of the Tufang and the offensive against the Qiongfang, and the southern expedition that secured the copper road against the Hufang and the Moufang.

Annotations

[§2] 约公元前1260年—公元前1235年

[§2] c. 1260–1235 BC

甲骨卜辞显示，商王武丁在统治的早期，逐一征服亘方、缶、基方、湔方等国，实现了对晋南地区的控制。

The oracle-bone inscriptions show that King Wu Ding of Shang, in the early part of his reign, conquered one after another the Gengfang, Fou, the Jifang, the Jianfang and other states, and gained control of the southern Shanxi region.

考古表明，进入晚商殷墟一期后，桓台史家遗址的土著文化因素被商文化所取代；殷墟甲骨卜辞中有一位叫做子画的王族成员，于武丁初年被封在东方，其封地也被称为画。部分学者认为子画受封是为了镇守商王朝新征服的土地。

Archaeology shows that with the Late Shang Yinxu Phase I the indigenous cultural elements at the Shijia site in Huantai were replaced by Shang culture; the Yinxu oracle-bone inscriptions have a member of the royal clan called Zi Hua, enfeoffed in the east in Wu Ding's first years, whose fief was likewise called Hua. Some scholars hold that Zi Hua was enfeoffed to guard the land the Shang court had newly conquered.

武丁在征讨晋南地区的方国后，继而与雀征讨沚方与辔方；按卜辞的方位描述，这两者皆与方相邻。戉在武丁统治的早期发动过叛乱，同样为雀所征讨。沚方在被商王朝征服后，其首领沚馘成为了武丁的臣属，替武丁守卫西疆，又出兵协助武丁讨伐巴方，并镇压东部的谭国叛乱。

After his campaigns against the fang states of southern Shanxi, Wu Ding went on, with Que, to campaign against the Zhifang and the Peifang; by the inscriptions' description of their position, both bordered the Qiongfang1. Yue rose in revolt early in Wu Ding's reign and was likewise put down by Que. After the Zhifang was conquered by the Shang court, its chief Zhi Guo became Wu Ding's vassal, guarding the western frontier for him, sending troops to help him attack the Bafang, and putting down the revolt of the state of Tan in the east.

在此之后，武丁又发动大军击败了方的盟友土方。武丁的配偶妇好是征伐土方的统帅之一，甲骨卜辞有“呼妇好伐土方”的记录；妇好也在武丁统治的早期有过征伐夷方、巴方等国的记载。武丁的另外一位配偶妇耕也曾出征龙方。

After this Wu Ding raised a great army and defeated the Qiongfang's ally, the Tufang. Wu Ding's consort Fu Zi was one of the commanders of the campaign against the Tufang; the oracle-bone inscriptions record "calling on Fu Zi to attack the Tufang2". Fu Zi is also recorded, in the early part of Wu Ding's reign, as campaigning against the Yifang, the Bafang and other states. Another consort of Wu Ding, Fu Geng, also went out against the Longfang.

在入侵商朝的方国中，大多数的方国侵扰地点分布虽广，但每次入侵的规模都比较小；然方则不然，不仅入侵频率高，造成的破坏也非常大，时常联合东面的土方一同进攻商王朝。部分学者认为方与考古意义上的石楼类型遗存有关，与其在中商时期便不断向晋中地区扩展势力或有一定联系。

Of the fang states that raided the Shang, most raided at widely scattered points but on a small scale each time; not so the Qiongfang, whose raids were frequent and highly destructive, and which often joined with the Tufang to its east in attacking the Shang court. Some scholars connect the Qiongfang with the remains of the Shilou type in archaeological terms, which may have some bearing on its steady extension of its power into the central Shanxi region from the Middle Shang period onward.

武丁在击败土方之后，开始对方展开攻势，然而在征伐的卜辞记载中，妇好并未见载；部分学者据此判断妇好去世于征土方之后，征方之前。

After defeating the Tufang, Wu Ding opened his offensive against the Qiongfang; but Fu Zi does not appear in the inscriptions recording that campaign, and some scholars conclude from this that she died after the campaign against the Tufang and before that against the Qiongfang.

在考古层面上，殷墟一期时汉江地区的商文化趋于消失，但产自南方的青铜资源仍然大量涌现于殷商统治中心，这说明商王朝仍然致力于维持对南方的部族进行间接控制；为了确保“铜路”的安全通畅，武丁在位时发起了南征行动，对虎方、方进行了讨伐。殷墟一期卜辞中有武丁和望乘征方的内容，此后其在卜辞记录中未再见载，推测已被消灭。

On the archaeological level, in Yinxu Phase I the Shang culture of the Hanjiang region was fading away, yet bronze metals from the south still flowed in quantity into the centre of Yin Shang rule, which shows that the Shang court was still at pains to keep indirect control over the peoples of the south; to secure the "copper road", Wu Ding in his reign launched a southern expedition and attacked the Hufang and the Moufang3. The Yinxu Phase I inscriptions record Wu Ding and Wang Cheng campaigning against the Moufang, which thereafter appears no more in the inscriptions and is presumed to have been destroyed.

Terms

oracle-bone inscriptions / 甲骨卜辞; 卜辞n. the divination records of the Shang kings on turtle plastron and ox bone.
the Gengfang / 亘方n. a fang state west of Yin, in southern Shanxi, conquered by Wu Ding; written 亙方 in some lists.
Fou / 缶n. the state of Fou (缶方) west of Yin, conquered by Wu Ding.
the Jifang / 基方n. a fang state west of Yin, placed at modern Puxian, Shanxi, conquered by Wu Ding.
the Jianfang / 湔方n. a fang state west of Yin, in the Shanxi–Shaanxi uplands, conquered by Wu Ding.
Late Shang / 晚商n. the Yinxu period of Shang archaeology, from Pan Geng's move to Yin.
Yinxu Phase I / 殷墟一期n. the first of the four phases of the Yinxu (Anyang) period, corresponding to the reigns of Pan Geng to Wu Ding.
fang state / 方n. the polities around the Shang, so named in the oracle-bone inscriptions.
the Zhifang / 沚方n. a fang state west of Yin, bordering the Qiongfang; conquered by Wu Ding, its chief Zhi Guo then his vassal.
the Peifang / 辔方n. a fang state west of Yin, bordering the Qiongfang, attacked by Wu Ding and Que.
the Qiongfang / 方n. the most persistent enemy of Wu Ding's Shang, north-west of Yin in the Shanxi–Shaanxi uplands, often allied with the Tufang; the graph , 工 over 口, is also transcribed 工方.
Yue / 戉n. a polity or leader in the west that revolted early in Wu Ding's reign and was put down by Que.
the Bafang / 巴方n. a fang state in Shanxi, attacked under Wu Ding.
the Tufang / 土方n. a fang state north-west of Yin, east of the Qiongfang, in the Fen valley of Shanxi; defeated by Wu Ding.
the Yifang / 夷方n. a fang state of the east, against which Fu Zi campaigned.
the Longfang / 龙方n. a fang state west of Yin, against which Fu Geng campaigned.
Shilou type / 石楼类型n. the late Shang bronze-bearing remains of the Shanxi–Shaanxi uplands named from Shilou County, Shanxi, which some connect with the Qiongfang.
Middle Shang / 中商n. the middle period of Shang archaeology, between the Erligang and Yinxu periods.
the copper road / 铜路n. the route by which copper and other bronze metals reached Yin from the south.
the Hufang / 虎方n. a fang state of the south, attacked in Wu Ding's southern expedition; identified by different scholars with the Miaotaizi type, the Dachengdun type or the Wucheng culture.
the Moufang / 方n. a fang state of the south, attacked by Wu Ding and Wang Cheng and thereafter unrecorded; its graph, 中 with a doubled vertical stroke, has no code point and is drawn.

Persons

Wu Ding 武丁King of Shang, son of Xiao Yi, whose reign opens the Yinxu period of the oracle-bone inscriptions.
Zi Hua 子画A member of the Shang royal clan in the oracle-bone inscriptions, enfeoffed in the east at Hua in Wu Ding's first years.
Que 雀A commander of Wu Ding's campaigns in the west, against the Zhifang, the Peifang and Yue.
Zhi Guo 沚馘Chief of the Zhifang after its conquest, Wu Ding's vassal on the western frontier.
Fu Zi 妇好Consort of Wu Ding and commander of his campaigns, against the Tufang among others; her tomb at Yinxu was found intact in 1976. The name is also read Fu Hao.
Fu Geng 妇耕A consort of Wu Ding who campaigned against the Longfang.
Wang Cheng 望乘A commander of Wu Ding, who campaigned with him against the Moufang.

Notes

1 the Qiongfang方: the graph is 工 over 口 (), which the site's font lacks, so it is drawn. The state is read Qiongfang; some transcribe it 工方.
2 calling on Fu Zi to attack the Tufang呼妇好伐土方: an oracle-bone formula, 呼 "call on" being the verb by which the king commands a subordinate to act; the inscription is the divination charge, not a narrative.
