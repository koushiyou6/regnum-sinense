约公元前1088年—公元前1046年

c. 1088–1046 BC

History of the Zhou Dynasty · Shang · 商

Introduction

From the death of Jili to the fall of Shang, about 1088 to 1046 BC: Di Yi and the opening of Yinxu Phase IV; Di Xin's campaigns against the Yifang and his strengthening of the royal power; King Wen's imprisonment at Youli, the receiving of the Mandate and his campaigns down to the move to Feng; and King Wu's review of arms at Meng Ford, the split within Yin Shang and the battle of Muye.

Annotations

[§10] 约公元前1088年—公元前1046年

[§10] c. 1088–1046 BC

周王季历遇害后，其子昌继任周国君主，即周文王。商王文丁也在不久后去世，其子帝乙即位，而晚商亦进入了最后一期：殷墟四期。

After Jili, King of Zhou, was killed, his son Chang succeeded him as ruler of the state of Zhou; he is King Wen of Zhou. Wen Ding, King of Shang, also died not long after, and his son Di Yi came to the throne; and the Late Shang entered its last phase, Yinxu Phase IV.

古本《竹书纪年》记载：“帝乙处殷，二年，周人伐商。”部分学者认为，这是季历被杀后商周矛盾激化的表现；至于周人此次伐商的结果，则因周人力量不足而不可能取得成功。于是受挫后的周文王重新检讨了内外政策，转而与殷商和好，并致力于扭转力量对比。

The old-text Bamboo Annals records: "Di Yi resided at Yin1. In his second year the men of Zhou attacked Shang." Some scholars hold that this shows the conflict between Shang and Zhou sharpening after Jili was killed; as for the outcome of this attack on Shang, the Zhou people's strength was not enough and it cannot have succeeded. And so King Wen of Zhou, after this setback, reviewed his policies at home and abroad afresh, turned to making peace with Yin Shang, and set himself to reversing the balance of strength.

就考古层面而言，进入殷墟四期后，商文化一方面在东部地区繁荣发展，另一方面则在西部地区日渐式微；在郑家坡文化与刘家文化融合形成了岐、丰地区的晚期先周文化后，原本与商文化联系紧密的晋中地区考古亦呈现出空白状。这一阶段，帝乙曾征伐过盂方。

On the archaeological level, after the opening of Yinxu Phase IV Shang culture on the one hand flourished in the east, and on the other dwindled day by day in the west; after the Zhengjiapo culture and the Liujia culture had merged to form the late Proto-Zhou culture of the Qi (岐) and Feng region, the archaeology of the central Shanxi region, once closely tied to Shang culture, likewise shows a blank. In this stage Di Yi campaigned against the Yufang.

淮夷文化承接大城墩四期文化发展。

The Huaiyi culture took over from the Dachengdun Phase IV culture and carried its development forward.

帝乙去世后，其子帝辛继位；在考古层面上，出土的宰椃角铭文示帝辛在位至少二十有五年，但目前尚未发现帝辛的统治末年大肆征伐东夷的考古证据。不过，在帝辛在位的前中期，确实有征夷方的卜辞以及相关金文记录。

After Di Yi's death his son Di Xin2 succeeded to the throne; on the archaeological level, the inscription of the unearthed Zai Hao jiao shows that Di Xin was on the throne for at least twenty-five years, but so far no archaeological evidence has been found of great campaigns against the Dongyi in the last years of Di Xin's rule. In the early and middle part of Di Xin's reign, however, there are indeed oracle-bone inscriptions on campaigning against the Yifang, and records in bronze inscriptions that bear on it.

帝辛征伐夷方的军事行动，已经一年多的筹划准备后进行的“十祀征夷方”最为著名；帝辛十祀九月甲午的卜辞显示帝辛准备与靠近夷方的攸国的侯喜一同征伐夷方，并在出征前于王畿“大邑商”举行了告祭仪式。

Of Di Xin's military operations against the Yifang, the best known is the "campaign against the Yifang in the tenth si", carried out after more than a year of planning and preparation; the inscription of Jiawu of September in Di Xin's tenth si shows Di Xin preparing to campaign against the Yifang together with Xi, Marquis of the state of You, which lay close to the Yifang, and holding, before setting out, a rite of announcement sacrifice in the royal domain, at the "Great Settlement Shang".

十祀十一月，帝辛抵达危地。十二月初，与攸侯喜会师，并从癸酉日开始和夷方交战。根据卜辞记载，十日后，夷方似乎一时退却。之后帝辛率军抵达了淮河沿岸并征服林方；到了正月的丙午日，商军再与夷方交战。胜利后，帝辛率军在十一祀的二月由攸国踏上归途回师。

In November of the tenth si Di Xin reached Wei (危). At the beginning of December he joined forces with Xi, Marquis of You, and from the day Guiyou began fighting the Yifang. By the record of the inscriptions, ten days later the Yifang seems for a time to have fallen back. After that Di Xin led his army to the banks of Huai River and conquered the Linfang; on Bingwu of January the Shang army fought the Yifang again. After the victory Di Xin, in February of the eleventh si, set out from You homebound with his army.

注：进军路线的“淮”亦可释为“潍”，故有两说。

Note: the "Huai" (淮) of the line of march can also be read as "Wei" (潍), so that there are two accounts.

除了“十祀征夷方”以外，帝辛还发动了“十五祀征夷方”，佐证来自商末小臣艅犀尊的铭文：“王赐小臣艅夔贝，隹王来征夷方，隹王十祀又五，彡日。”另有几则年月不明的商末卜辞及金文材料亦与征夷方相关。

Besides the "campaign against the Yifang in the tenth si", Di Xin also launched a "campaign against the Yifang in the fifteenth si"; the evidence comes from the inscription of the rhinoceros zun of the Lesser Minister Yu3, of the end of Shang: "The King bestowed on the Lesser Minister Yu cowries of Kui. It was when the King came back from campaigning against the Yifang; it was the King's fifteenth si, the day of the rong sacrifice." Several further oracle-bone and bronze inscriptions of the end of Shang, their year and month unknown, also bear on the campaigns against the Yifang.

在对外用兵的同时，帝辛也对内加强王权，严格地奉行祭祀先王、先妣的“周祭制度”；与其他奉行周祭制度的先王相比，帝辛为了集中王权，将打压旁系宗亲从祭祀层面延伸到了政治层面，加大重用小臣的力度并疏远旧贵，黄组卜辞中小臣任事内容多于旧贵职官，可为佐证。如此一来，商朝政坛上长期以来的平衡被打破，导致了商王朝固有的制度运转不畅，破坏了政治体系的稳定性。

While using arms abroad, Di Xin also strengthened the royal power at home, strictly keeping up the "cyclical sacrifice system" of sacrifices to the former kings and their consorts; compared with the other former kings who kept the cyclical sacrifice system, Di Xin, in order to concentrate the royal power, carried the suppression of the collateral branches of the royal clan from the level of sacrifice over to the level of politics, made ever greater use of Lesser Ministers and kept the old nobility at a distance, as is borne out by the Huang-group inscriptions, in which the employment of Lesser Ministers on affairs outweighs the offices of the old nobility. In this way the balance long kept in the politics of the Shang was broken, the established institutions of the Shang court ceased to run smoothly, and the stability of the political system was undermined.

另外，帝辛亦多接纳四方逃亡人口，加剧了与各族、各方国的矛盾，周文王则反其道而行之，明确提出了“有亡荒阅”政策，即搜捕逃亡人口，送还原主，借此笼络了殷商国内各族与附属方国。

In addition, Di Xin took in many fugitives from every quarter, which sharpened his conflicts with the clans and the fang states; King Wen of Zhou did the opposite, and expressly put forward the policy "Make great inquisition for fugitives4", that is, to hunt down fugitives and send them back to their masters, by which he won over the clans within Yin Shang and its dependent fang states.

据传世文献记载，在周国发展蒸蒸日上时，周王昌被帝辛诱捕囚禁于羑里。后来，经周国大臣七年间的收买与进献，文王才得以被释放回国，并被帝辛命为“西伯”。

According to the transmitted texts, while the state of Zhou was thriving, Chang, King of Zhou, was lured, seized and imprisoned at Youli by Di Xin. Later, after seven years of bribes and gifts from the ministers of Zhou, King Wen was at last released to return to his state, and was appointed "Chief of the West5" by Di Xin.

据《史记·周本纪》记载，虞、芮两国发生了争端，相持不下，于是找周文王裁决。两国之君抵达周国后，见民风崇尚礼让，便惭愧而归，不再相争。此事很快传播开来，诸侯皆曰：“西伯盖受命之君！”部分学者认为文王决断“虞芮争讼”，是一种发动宗亲与国，宣扬“受命”舆论的手段。

According to the Shiji, Basic Annals of Zhou, a dispute arose between the two states of Yu (虞) and Rui, neither of which would give way, and so they went to King Wen of Zhou for a ruling. When the rulers of the two states arrived in Zhou and saw that the ways of the people prized courtesy and yielding, they went home ashamed and contended no more. The matter soon spread abroad, and the Lords all said: "The Chief of the West must be the ruler who has received the Mandate!" Some scholars hold that King Wen's settling of the "suit of Yu and Rui" was a means of rousing his kinsmen and allied states and of spreading abroad the talk of his "receiving the Mandate".

据《逸周书·程寤篇》记载，文王因夫人太姒奇异的梦境而举行禳灾的仪式，与太子发“拜受吉梦”，以表示从“皇上帝”接收了殷商天命；周人因而视此年为“受命之年”。史载文王在“受命之年”决断“虞芮争讼”，第二年讨伐犬戎，第三年伐密须，并在灭密须的次年讨伐了耆国。文王在伐耆之后又伐邘，阻断了殷商王畿西部的天然屏障。

According to the Yi Zhou Shu, Awakening at Cheng, King Wen, because of a strange dream of his consort Tai Si, held a rite to avert calamity and, with the Heir Apparent Fa, "bowed and received the auspicious dream", to show that he had received from the "August High God" the Mandate of Heaven that had been Yin Shang's; the Zhou people therefore regarded this year as the "year of receiving the Mandate". History records that in the "year of receiving the Mandate" King Wen settled the "suit of Yu and Rui", in the second year attacked the Quan Rong, in the third year attacked Mixu, and in the year after destroying Mixu attacked the state of Qi (耆). After attacking Qi, King Wen went on to attack Yu (邘), cutting off the natural barrier west of Shang.

殷商旧贵和帝辛之间的矛盾日趋激化，而忙于压服国内贵族的帝辛，或许一时无法对周人的行动做出反应；山西地区的众多殷商属国，如吕国等，大致也在这一时期倒向了周人。

The conflict between the old nobility of Yin Shang and Di Xin grew sharper by the day, and Di Xin, busy forcing the nobles at home into submission, was perhaps for the time unable to respond to what the Zhou people were doing; the many dependent states of Yin Shang in the Shanxi region, the state of Lü and others, also went over to the Zhou people at about this time.

周文王的下一个目标，则是打通前往东方的主通道，扫除崇国；西安市灞桥区的老牛坡遗址被部分学者认为属于崇国，而根据碳十四测年结果，该遗址约在商末遭到废弃，遗址内的中型木椁墓被严重破坏，无一幸免，墓主遗骸遭到扬弃，随葬品十有八九皆无存。这或许是周人对崇侯虎及其宗族的一种报复行为。

King Wen of Zhou's next aim was to open the main road to the east and sweep away the state of Chong; the Laoniupo site in Baqiao District, Xi'an, is held by some scholars to belong to the state of Chong, and by the results of radiocarbon dating the site was abandoned about the end of Shang; the medium-sized tombs with wooden chambers within the site were badly wrecked, not one being spared, the remains of the tomb occupants were thrown out, and of the grave goods eight or nine parts in ten are gone. This was perhaps an act of revenge by the Zhou people on Hu, Marquis of Chong, and his clan.

根据上博楚简《容成氏》记载，文王降伏的邦国还有丰、镐、郍、鹿等；伊、洛以及豫西地区，大致在这一时期被周文王纳入了势力范围。一系列征伐行动结束后，周文王迁都丰邑，并于第二年去世。周文王去世后，太子发继位，是为周武王。

According to the Rongchengshi of the Shanghai Museum Chu slips, the states subdued by King Wen also included Feng, Hao, Nuo, Lu and others; Yi River, Luo River and the western Henan region were brought into King Wen of Zhou's sphere of power at about this time. When this series of campaigns was over King Wen of Zhou moved his capital to the settlement of Feng, and died the year after. After King Wen of Zhou's death the Heir Apparent Fa succeeded to the throne; he is King Wu of Zhou.

据传世文献记载，武王即王位数年后大举起兵攻商，带着文王的神位声言奉父命出征，又派人联络各地氏族部落在盟津观兵；部分学者认为文王受命七年而崩，又认为武王沿用受命纪年，未曾改元。按此推算，则《史记》中观兵盟津的“九年”，实际是武王在位的第二年。另一批学者持反对意见，指出先秦文献中记载武王实有“元祀”等独立纪年。

According to the transmitted texts, some years after coming to the throne King Wu raised a great army to attack Shang, carrying King Wen's spirit tablet and declaring that he marched at his father's command, and sent men to bring the clans and tribes of every region together for a review of arms at Meng Ford; some scholars hold that King Wen died seven years after receiving the Mandate, and that King Wu went on with the count of years from the Mandate and never began a count of his own. Reckoned in this way, the "ninth year" of the review of arms at Meng Ford in the Shiji is in fact the second year of King Wu's reign. Another group of scholars takes the opposite view, pointing out that the pre-Qin texts record King Wu as having a count of years of his own, such as a "first si".

按“武王改元说”的观点，从武王即位到观兵盟津足有九年之久，在此期间，周人放缓了文王后期的持续扩张的势头，转而经营丰邑、建造镐京；至于商朝方面，在周人势力已逼近王畿的情况下，即使帝辛需要解决国内宗亲旧族离心的问题，也不得不腾出手来对周人采取反制措施了。

On the view that "King Wu began a count of his own", fully nine years passed from King Wu's accession to the review of arms at Meng Ford, and in this time the Zhou people slackened the continuous expansion of King Wen's later years and turned to the ordering of the settlement of Feng and the building of Haojing; as for the Shang, with the power of the Zhou people already pressing on the royal domain, Di Xin, even though he had the disaffection of his kinsmen and the old clans at home to deal with, had no choice but to free his hands and take countermeasures against the Zhou people.

在《左传》中提及的“黎之蒐”，或许就是帝辛为应对周邦攻势而采取的军事部署；帝辛将军队开到耆（黎）国，举行阅兵、组织演习，借此彰显武力，使耆国重新倒向殷商。

The "review of Li6" mentioned in the Zuozhuan was perhaps the military disposition Di Xin made in answer to the Zhou state's offensive; Di Xin moved his army up to the state of Qi (Li), held a review of troops and organised manoeuvres, and by this show of force brought the state of Qi back over to Yin Shang.

若采信《左传》的“黎之蒐”，那么后续的“东夷叛之”则亦应该发生在帝辛统治的后期；考古证据表明，几则无纪年的“征夷方”金文材料不排除可将商夷战争持续至帝辛二十一祀乃至更晚的可能性。如果以上推论成立，那么发生在“黎之蒐”后不久的东夷反叛，很可能扰乱了帝辛的计划，导致无法进一步化解周人势力对王畿的威胁。

If the "review of Li" of the Zuozhuan is accepted, then "the Dongyi revolted from him" that follows it should likewise have happened in the later part of Di Xin's rule; the archaeological evidence shows that several undated bronze inscriptions on "campaigning against the Yifang" do not rule out the possibility that the war between Shang and the Yifang went on to Di Xin's twenty-first si or even later. If the above inference holds, then the revolt of the Dongyi, coming not long after the "review of Li", very probably upset Di Xin's plans, so that he could do nothing further to remove the threat of the Zhou people's power to the royal domain.

另一方面，周人也不甘于失去对黎国的控制，所以在完成内部整合、准备充分后，周人再度征伐黎国，将其一举讨平；清华简《耆夜》有载：“武王八年，征伐耆，大戡之。”

On the other side, the Zhou people for their part were not willing to lose control of the state of Li, and so, once their internal consolidation was complete and their preparations fully made, the Zhou people campaigned against the state of Li a second time and put it down at one stroke; the Qiye of the Tsinghua slips records: "In King Wu's eighth year he campaigned against Qi and greatly conquered it."

据《史记·周本纪》记载：武王观兵盟津，“不期而会盟津者八百诸侯”，参加会盟的诸侯纷纷要求立刻伐纣灭商，武王对他们说：“女未知天命，未可也。”于是便率军返回；部分学者认为，武王这次“观兵盟津”有演习性质，以便熟悉路程和地形，将来能以洛师作为前进基地，顺利渡河北上。

According to the Shiji, Basic Annals of Zhou: when King Wu reviewed his arms at Meng Ford, "the Lords who met at Meng Ford without appointment were eight hundred"; the Lords who took part in the meeting one after another demanded that Zhou (纣) be attacked and Shang destroyed at once, and King Wu said to them: "You do not yet know the Mandate of Heaven; it cannot be done yet." And with that he led his army back; some scholars hold that this "review of arms at Meng Ford" of King Wu's was in the nature of a manoeuvre, to learn the road and the ground, so that in future he could use Luoshi as a forward base and cross the river northwards without difficulty.

据传世文献记载，武王“观兵盟津”后不久，殷商统治集团内部出现了严重的撕裂。帝辛为压制反对自己的贵族，采取了非常极端的手段；最终的结果是：王子比干被杀，箕子被囚，太师疵、少师彊等人直接投奔周国。武王认为时机已经成熟，便通告同盟诸侯：“殷有重罪，不可以不毕伐！”

According to the transmitted texts, not long after King Wu's "review of arms at Meng Ford" a grave split appeared within the ruling group of Yin Shang. To hold down the nobles who opposed him, Di Xin took very extreme measures; the final outcome was that Prince Bigan was killed, Jizi was imprisoned, and the Grand Preceptor Ci, the Junior Preceptor Qiang and others went straight over to the state of Zhou. King Wu judged that the time was ripe, and proclaimed to the allied Lords: "Yin has committed grave crimes; it cannot but be attacked to the end!"

武王十一年一月癸巳日，周军率领戎车三百、虎贲三千、甲士四万五千人正式出发；同盟方国的军队前来与周军会合，武王作《太誓》以告诸侯、众士。周军在戊午日渡过黄河后，于二月癸亥日夜间抵达殷商别都朝歌郊外的牧野；据《国语·周语》记载：癸亥日的夜里下了一场雨，周军冒雨列阵，第二天甲子日的拂晓，武王在阵前作《牧誓》，宣告帝辛罪状。

On Guisi of January in King Wu's eleventh year the Zhou army formally set out, with three hundred war chariots, three thousand Tiger Braves and forty-five thousand armoured men; the armies of the allied fang states came to join the Zhou army, and King Wu made the Great Declaration7 to address the Lords and all the gentlemen. After crossing the Yellow River on the day Wuwu, the Zhou army reached Muye, outside Zhaoge, the secondary capital of Yin Shang, in the night of Guihai of February; according to the Guoyu, Discourses of Zhou, rain fell in the night of Guihai and the Zhou army drew up its lines in the rain, and at daybreak the next day, Jiazi, King Wu made the Speech at Mu before the lines, proclaiming Di Xin's crimes.

按照《逸周书·克殷》所载，在牧野之战中，帝辛的军队被周军精锐冲垮而败北，帝辛于甲子日当晚于鹿台自焚；亦有说法认为帝辛被生擒后斩杀。

By the account of the Yi Zhou Shu, Conquest of Yin, in the battle of Muye Di Xin's army was broken by the charge of the picked troops of Zhou and was defeated, and Di Xin burned himself to death on Lu Terrace on the evening of that same day, Jiazi; there is also an account that Di Xin was taken alive and then beheaded.

Terms

Late Shang / 晚商n. the Yinxu period of Shang archaeology, from Pan Geng's move to Yin.
Yinxu Phase IV / 殷墟四期n. the last of the four phases of the Yinxu (Anyang) period, opening with the accession of Di Yi.
Zhengjiapo culture / 郑家坡文化n. the late Shang-period culture of the Qishui valley named from the Zhengjiapo site, Wugong, Shaanxi.
Liujia culture / 刘家文化n. the Late Shang-period culture of the western Guanzhong named from the Liujia cemetery, Fufeng, Shaanxi; regarded by many scholars as the culture of the Qiangfang.
Proto-Zhou culture / 先周文化n. the archaeological culture of the Zhou before the conquest of Shang.
the Yufang / 盂方n. a fang state against which Di Yi campaigned.
Huaiyi culture / 淮夷文化n. the culture of the Huai River region that followed the Dachengdun Phase IV culture.
Dachengdun Phase IV culture / 大城墩四期文化n. the culture named from the fourth phase of the Dachengdun site, Hanshan, Anhui.
Zai Hao jiao / 宰椃角n. a bronze jiao of the end of Shang, made for the steward (宰) Hao 椃, whose inscription is dated to the king's twenty-fifth si.
the Dongyi / 东夷n. the Yi peoples of the east, in the transmitted texts.
oracle-bone inscriptions / 甲骨卜辞; 卜辞n. the divination records of the Shang kings on turtle plastron and ox bone.
the Yifang / 夷方n. a fang state of the east, against which Fu Zi campaigned.
bronze inscriptions / 金文n. inscriptions cast or cut on bronze vessels.
si / 祀n. the ritual year of the late Shang kings, a full round of the cyclical sacrifices, by which they counted the years of a reign.
announcement sacrifice / 告祭n. a sacrifice at which an undertaking is announced to the ancestors.
royal domain / 王畿n. the land about the capital under the king's own rule.
the Linfang / 林方n. a fang state conquered by Di Xin on his campaign of the tenth si.
rhinoceros zun of the Lesser Minister Yu / 小臣艅犀尊n. a bronze zun in the shape of a rhinoceros, of the end of Shang, with an inscription of twenty-seven characters; now in the Asian Art Museum, San Francisco.
cyclical sacrifice system / 周祭制度n. the fixed round of sacrifices offered in turn to the royal ancestors of the Shang, each on the day of his or her stem name.
Lesser Minister / 小臣n. xiaochen, an official in the king's personal service.
Huang-group inscriptions / 黄组卜辞n. the latest group of the Yinxu oracle-bone inscriptions, named from the diviner Huang 黄.
fang state / 方n. the polities around the Shang, so named in the oracle-bone inscriptions.
Chief of the West / 西伯n. the title King Wen held under Di Xin.
the Lords / 诸侯npl. the rulers of the states, collectively; in this period the kings of the warring states and the lesser lords, as distinct from the Zhou king.
receiving the Mandate / 受命n. the receiving of the Mandate of Heaven to rule, claimed for King Wen.
Heir Apparent / 太子n. the designated successor of a king.
High God / 上帝n. the supreme deity, Shangdi, to whom the suburban sacrifice is made.
the Quan Rong / 犬戎n. a Rong people attacked by King Wen.
Shanghai Museum Chu slips / 上博楚简n. the Warring States bamboo manuscripts of Chu held by the Shanghai Museum.
Tsinghua slips / 清华简n. the Warring States bamboo manuscripts held by Tsinghua University.
Tiger Braves / 虎贲n. huben, the picked warriors of the king's guard.
