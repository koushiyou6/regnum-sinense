c. 1150 BC

History of the Zhou Dynasty · Shang · 商

Introduction

The reigns of Lin Xin and Kang Ding, about 1150 BC: the Qiangfang's threat to the western lands and the eastward advance of the Liujia culture, the Zaoshugounao and Heidouzui types of the Chunhua region and the question of the Fang and the Xunyu, and the Qinghai Bronze and Upper Zhangjiayuan cultures.

Annotations

[§7] 约公元前1150年

[§7] c. 1150 BC

祖甲去世后，其子廪辛、康丁先后继位。而与相对和平的祖甲时期不同，涉及战争的卜辞内容在廪辛、康丁时期重新变得频繁起来。到祖甲之子廪辛即位时，日益壮大的羌方已开始对商王朝的西土造成了严重的威胁；随着刘家文化的强势东进，不仅是商文化退出了关中西部，刚有发展势头的郑家坡文化也受到了冲击。

After Zu Jia's death his sons Lin Xin and Kang Ding succeeded in turn. Unlike the relatively peaceful time of Zu Jia, inscriptions concerned with war became frequent again under Lin Xin and Kang Ding. By the accession of Zu Jia's son Lin Xin, the ever-growing Qiangfang had begun to pose a serious threat to the western lands of the Shang court; with the strong eastward advance of the Liujia culture, not only did Shang culture withdraw from the western Guanzhong, but the Zhengjiapo culture, which had only just begun to gather momentum, was hit as well.

考古表明，殷墟二、三期之交，晚期朱开沟文化与李家崖文化因素向南传播，与关中文化交融形成了淳化地区的枣树沟脑类型和黑豆嘴类型遗存。部分学者认为这或许与廪辛、康丁卜辞中再度壮大的方有所关联；在祖甲时期的卜辞中，涉及战争的内容虽然很少，但几乎都和方的侵扰相关。到了廪辛、康丁在位时期，其侵扰亦频次加剧。也有学者主张将其与传世文献中逼迫周太王从豳地南迁至岐周的“薰鬻戎狄”对应起来。

Archaeology shows that at the turn from Yinxu Phase II to Phase III, elements of the late Zhukaigou culture and the Lijiaya culture spread south and, blending with the cultures of Guanzhong, formed the remains of the Zaoshugounao type and the Heidouzui type in the Chunhua region. Some scholars think this may be connected with the Fang, grown strong once more in the inscriptions of Lin Xin and Kang Ding; in the inscriptions of Zu Jia's time what concerns war is very little, yet nearly all of it has to do with the raids of the Fang. By the reigns of Lin Xin and Kang Ding its raids, too, had grown more frequent. Other scholars propose to identify it with the "Xunyu Rong and Di"1 of the transmitted texts, who forced King Tai of Zhou to move south from Bin to Qi Zhou.

青海青铜文化进入中期晚段；张家园上层文化兴起。

The Qinghai Bronze culture entered the late stage of its middle period; the Upper Zhangjiayuan culture arose.

Terms

oracle-bone inscriptions / 甲骨卜辞; 卜辞n. the divination records of the Shang kings on turtle plastron and ox bone.
the Qiangfang / 羌方n. the Qiang as a fang state of the west in the oracle-bone inscriptions.
the western lands / 西土n. the lands west of the Shang, as the oracle-bone inscriptions call them.
Liujia culture / 刘家文化n. the Late Shang-period culture of the western Guanzhong named from the Liujia cemetery, Fufeng, Shaanxi; regarded by many scholars as the culture of the Qiangfang.
Zhengjiapo culture / 郑家坡文化n. the late Shang-period culture of the Qishui valley named from the Zhengjiapo site, Wugong, Shaanxi.
Zhukaigou / 朱开沟n. the Bronze Age culture of the Ordos named from the Zhukaigou site, Ejin Horo Banner, and the people who bore it.
Lijiaya culture / 李家崖文化n. the Late Shang-period culture of northern Shaanxi and the facing bank of the Yellow River, named from the Lijiaya site, Qingjian, Shaanxi.
Zaoshugounao type / 枣树沟脑类型n. a type of remains in the Chunhua region named from the Zaoshugounao site, formed as late Zhukaigou and Lijiaya elements blended with the cultures of Guanzhong.
Heidouzui type / 黑豆嘴类型n. a type of remains in the Chunhua region named from the Heidouzui site, formed as late Zhukaigou and Lijiaya elements blended with the cultures of Guanzhong.
the Fang / 方方n. the fang state named Fang 方 in the oracle-bone inscriptions, a people of the north-west under Wu Ding.
the Xunyu Rong and Di / 薰鬻戎狄n. the Xunyu, the northern people who in the transmitted texts pressed on the Zhou under King Tai; written 薰育 in the Basic Annals of Zhou and 獯鬻 in the Mencius.
Qinghai Bronze culture / 青海青铜文化n. the Bronze Age culture of Qinghai.
Upper Zhangjiayuan culture / 张家园上层文化n. the culture named from the upper stratum of the Zhangjiayuan site, Jizhou, Tianjin.

Persons

Zu Jia 祖甲King of Shang, youngest son of Wu Ding and successor of his brother Zu Geng.
Lin Xin 廪辛King of Shang, son and successor of Zu Jia.
Kang Ding 康丁King of Shang, son of Zu Jia and successor of his brother Lin Xin.
King Tai of Zhou 周太王Gugong Danfu 古公亶父, ancestor of the Zhou kings, who moved the Zhou from Bin to the foot of Mount Qi.

Places

Shang 商the Shang state and dynasty, with its late capital at Yin, modern Anyang, Henan.
Guanzhong 关中the Wei River valley "within the passes", the heartland of Qin; modern central Shaanxi.
Chunhua 淳化Chunhua County, Xianyang, Shaanxi, on the northern edge of the Guanzhong.
Bin 豳the seat of the Zhou before King Tai's move; traditionally placed about Xunyi and Binzhou, Shaanxi.
Qi Zhou 岐周the seat of the Zhou at the foot of Mount Qi, in the Zhouyuan, from King Tai's move.

Notes

1 the Xunyu Rong and Di薰鬻戎狄: the Basic Annals of Zhou have the 薰育戎狄 attack Gugong Danfu, who is King Tai; the Mencius writes 獯鬻.
