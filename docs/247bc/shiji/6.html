Ivar the Boneless' commentary and analysis I

Shiji 史记 · 247 BC · 秦庄襄王三年

Introduction

Ivar the Boneless' commentary on the year: the attacks of Wei, Chu and Yan on Qi, Tian Dan's siege of Liaocheng, and Chu and Wei's turn from Qi to an alliance against Qin, each with the Biographies of Lu Zhonglian and Zou Yang adduced as the probable source, their date left open; and the passage itself, Lu Zhonglian's refusal of reward and his letter to the general of Yan in Liaocheng.

[§6] 无骨论点补充（以及辨析）一

[§6] Ivar the Boneless' commentary and analysis I

魏、楚、燕攻齐;魏取平陆，楚取齐南阳，燕取聊城。

Wei, Chu and Yan attacked Qi: Wei took Pinglu, Chu took Qi's Nanyang, and Yan took Liaocheng.

辨析：此條出處或是《史記·鲁仲连邹阳列传》。然而原文時間只有信陵君竊符救趙的“其後二十馀年”，具體時間點不詳。

Analysis: the source of this item is perhaps the Shiji, Biographies of Lu Zhonglian and Zou Yang. But the only date in the text is "twenty-odd years after"1 Lord Xinling's theft of the tally to save Zhao, and the exact point in time is not known.

齐安平君田单率军围攻聊城。

Tian Dan, Lord Anping of Qi, led an army to besiege Liaocheng.

辨析：此條出處或是《史記·鲁仲连邹阳列传》。然而原文時間只有信陵君竊符救趙的“其後二十馀年”，具體時間點不詳。

Analysis: the source of this item is perhaps the Shiji, Biographies of Lu Zhonglian and Zou Yang. But the only date in the text is "twenty-odd years after" Lord Xinling's theft of the tally to save Zhao, and the exact point in time is not known.

楚、魏两国放弃攻齐，谋划合纵攻秦。

Chu and Wei gave up attacking Qi and planned a Vertical Alliance to attack Qin.

辨析：此條出處或是《史記·鲁仲连邹阳列传》。然而原文時間只有信陵君竊符救趙的“其後二十馀年”，具體時間點不詳。

Analysis: the source of this item is perhaps the Shiji, Biographies of Lu Zhonglian and Zou Yang. But the only date in the text is "twenty-odd years after" Lord Xinling's theft of the tally to save Zhao, and the exact point in time is not known.

《史記·鲁仲连邹阳列传》：適会魏公子无忌夺晋鄙军以救赵，击秦军，秦军遂引而去。

Shiji, Biographies of Lu Zhonglian and Zou Yang: It happened just then2 that the Prince of Wei, Wuji, seized Jin Bi's army to relieve Zhao and attacked the Qin army, and the Qin army withdrew.

於是平原君欲封鲁连，鲁连辞让者三，终不肯受。平原君乃置酒，酒酣起前，以千金为鲁连寿。鲁连笑曰：“所贵於天下之士者，为人排患释难解纷乱而无取也。即有取者，是商贾之事也，而连不忍为也。”遂辞平原君而去，终身不复见。

Thereupon Lord Pingyuan wished to enfeoff Lu Lian; Lu Lian declined three times and in the end would not accept. Lord Pingyuan then gave a banquet, and when the wine was flowing he rose, came forward and offered Lu Lian a thousand jin of gold for his long life. Lu Lian laughed and said: "What is prized in a gentleman of all under Heaven is that he removes troubles, resolves difficulties and settles disorders for others, and takes nothing for it. If he takes something, that is the business of merchants, and Lian cannot bring himself to do it." So he took leave of Lord Pingyuan and departed, and never saw him again as long as he lived.

其後二十馀年，燕将攻下聊城，聊城人或谗之燕，燕将惧诛，因保守聊城，不敢归。齐田单攻聊城岁馀，士卒多死而聊城不下。鲁连乃为书，约之矢以射城中，遗燕将。书曰：

Twenty-odd years later, a general of Yan attacked and took Liaocheng. Someone of Liaocheng slandered him to Yan, and the general of Yan, fearing execution, held Liaocheng and did not dare return. Tian Dan of Qi attacked Liaocheng3 for more than a year; many of his soldiers died, yet Liaocheng did not fall. Lu Lian then wrote a letter, tied it to an arrow and shot it into the Fortress, to be delivered to the general of Yan. The letter said:

吾闻之，智者不倍时而弃利，勇士不却死而灭名，忠臣不先身而後君。今公行一朝之忿，不顾燕王之无臣，非忠也；杀身亡聊城，而威不信於齐，非勇也；功败名灭，後世无称焉，非智也。三者世主不臣，说士不载，故智者不再计，勇士不怯死。今死生荣辱，贵贱尊卑，此时不再至，原公详计而无与俗同。

"I have heard that the wise man does not turn his back on the hour and throw away advantage, the brave man does not shun death4 and destroy his name, and the loyal minister does not put himself first and his lord after. Now you are acting on a single morning's anger, with no thought for the King of Yan's being left without his minister: that is not loyalty. To be killed and lose Liaocheng, your might unproven against Qi: that is not courage. With your deeds undone and your name wiped out, and nothing for later ages to tell of: that is not wisdom. Men of these three kinds the rulers of the age do not take as ministers, and the persuaders do not record; therefore the wise man does not deliberate twice, and the brave man does not flinch from death. Now life and death, glory and disgrace, high and low, honour and baseness: this hour will not come again. I wish you to consider it carefully and not go along with the common run.

且楚攻齐之南阳，魏攻平陆，而齐无南面之心，以为亡南阳之害小，不如得济北之利大，故定计审处之。今秦人下兵，魏不敢东面；衡秦之势成，楚国之形危；齐弃南阳，断右壤，定济北，计犹且为之也。且夫齐之必决於聊城，公勿再计。今楚魏交退於齐，而燕救不至。以全齐之兵，无天下之规，与聊城共据期年之敝，则臣见公之不能得也。且燕国大乱，君臣失计，上下迷惑，栗腹以十万之众五折於外，以万乘之国被围於赵，壤削主困，为天下僇笑。国敝而祸多，民无所归心。今公又以敝聊之民距全齐之兵，是墨翟之守也。食人炊骨，士无反外之心，是孙膑之兵也。能见於天下。虽然，为公计者，不如全车甲以报於燕。车甲全而归燕，燕王必喜；身全而归於国，士民如见父母，交游攘臂而议於世，功业可明。上辅孤主以制群臣，下养百姓以资说士，矫国更俗，功名可立也。亡意亦捐燕弃世，东游於齐乎？裂地定封，富比乎陶、卫，世世称孤，与齐久存，又一计也。此两计者，显名厚实也，原公详计而审处一焉。

"Moreover, Chu attacked Qi's Nanyang and Wei attacked Pinglu, yet Qi has no mind to turn south5: it holds the harm of losing Nanyang to be small, not to compare with the great gain of winning Jibei, and so it has fixed its plan and deals with the matter accordingly. Now the men of Qin have sent down their troops and Wei dares not face east; the Horizontal Alliance with Qin is formed6 and the position of the state of Chu is in danger; Qi gives up Nanyang, cuts off its right-hand lands and settles Jibei7: its plan is still to go through with it. And since Qi is set on deciding the matter at Liaocheng, do not deliberate again. Now Chu and Wei have both withdrawn from Qi8, and the relief from Yan does not come. Against the whole strength of Qi, with no design from all under Heaven, holding out with Liaocheng in its exhaustion of a full year: your servant sees that you cannot succeed. Moreover, the state of Yan is in great disorder, ruler and ministers have lost their counsel, high and low are bewildered; Li Fu9 with a host of a hundred thousand was broken five times abroad, a state of ten thousand chariots was besieged by Zhao, its land pared away and its ruler in straits, the laughing-stock of all under Heaven. The state is exhausted and its troubles many, and the people have no one to turn to. Now you, with the people of worn-out Liao, hold off the whole strength of Qi: that is the defence of Mo Di10. Eating men and cooking bones, and the soldiers have no thought of turning against you: those are the troops of Sun Bin11. Your ability is seen by all under Heaven. Even so, if I were to plan for you, it would be better to bring your chariots and armour back whole and report to Yan. If the chariots and armour return whole to Yan, the King of Yan will surely rejoice; if you return in person whole to your state, the soldiers and people will greet you as a father and mother, your friends will bare their arms and speak of you to the age, and your deeds will be made plain. Above you will support an orphaned ruler in controlling the body of ministers; below you will nourish the hundred clans and furnish the persuaders12; you will set the state right and change its customs13, and your name and deeds can be established. Or if you have no mind for that14, will you not abandon Yan, cast off the age and go east to Qi? Land would be carved out and a fief fixed for you, with wealth to match Tao and Wey15; generation after generation you would call yourselves lords16, enduring as long as Qi: that is another plan. These two plans bring an illustrious name and solid substance. I wish you to consider them carefully and to settle on one.

且吾闻之，规小节者不能成荣名，恶小耻者不能立大功。昔者管夷吾射桓公中其钩，篡也；遗公子纠不能死，怯也；束缚桎梏，辱也。若此三行者，世主不臣而乡里不通。乡使管子幽囚而不出，身死而不反於齐，则亦名不免为辱人贱行矣。臧获且羞与之同名矣，况世俗乎！故管子不耻身在縲绁之中而耻天下之不治，不耻不死公子纠而耻威之不信於诸侯，故兼三行之过而为五霸首，名高天下而光烛邻国。曹子为鲁将，三战三北，而亡地五百里。乡使曹子计不反顾，议不还踵，刎颈而死，则亦名不免为败军禽将矣。曹子弃三北之耻，而退与鲁君计。桓公朝天下，会诸侯，曹子以一剑之任，枝桓公之心於坛坫之上，颜色不变，辞气不悖，三战之所亡一朝而复之，天下震动，诸侯惊骇，威加吴、越。若此二士者，非不能成小廉而行小节也，以为杀身亡躯，绝世灭後，功名不立，非智也。故去感忿之怨，立终身之名；弃忿悁之节，定累世之功。是以业与三王争流，而名与天壤相弊也。原公择一而行之。

"Moreover, I have heard that he who is scrupulous over small points cannot achieve a glorious name, and he who shrinks from small shames cannot establish great deeds. In former days Guan Yiwu shot at Duke Huan and hit the hook of his belt: that was usurpation; he abandoned Prince Jiu17 and could not die for him: that was cowardice; he was bound in fetters and shackles: that was disgrace. A man of these three conducts the rulers of the age do not take as minister, and his own village does not receive. Had Master Guan stayed shut in prison and never come out, died and never returned to Qi, his name too could not have escaped being that of a disgraced man of base conduct. Slaves and bondwomen18 would have been ashamed to share a name with him, how much more the common run of the age! But Master Guan was not ashamed to be in bonds, he was ashamed that all under Heaven was not in order; he was not ashamed not to have died for Prince Jiu, he was ashamed that his might was unproven among the Lords; and so, carrying the faults of three conducts, he became the first of the Five Hegemons19, his name high in all under Heaven and his light shining on the neighbouring states. Master Cao20, as general of Lu, fought three battles and was three times routed, and lost five hundred li of land. Had Master Cao not looked back in his counsels nor turned on his heel in his deliberations, but cut his throat and died, his name too could not have escaped being that of a defeated general, a captured commander. Master Cao put aside the shame of his three routs and withdrew to take counsel with the lord of Lu. When Duke Huan was receiving the homage of all under Heaven and assembling the Lords, Master Cao, trusting to a single sword, held it to Duke Huan's breast21 upon the altar mound; his colour did not change, his speech did not falter, and what three battles had lost he recovered in a single morning; all under Heaven was shaken, the Lords were struck with fear, and his might reached to Wu and Yue. These two gentlemen were not unable to achieve small scruples and carry out small points; they held that to be killed and lose one's body, to cut off one's line and destroy one's posterity, with no name or deed established, is not wisdom. Therefore they set aside the resentments of sudden anger and established a lifelong name; they cast off the points of indignant honour and fixed deeds for generations to come. Thus their achievements vie in the current with the Three Kings', and their names will wear out only with heaven and earth. I wish you to choose one of these and act on it."

燕将见鲁连书，泣三日，犹豫不能自决。欲归燕，已有隙，恐诛；欲降齐，所杀虏於齐甚众，恐已降而後见辱。喟然叹曰：“与人刃我，宁自刃。”乃自杀。聊城乱，田单遂屠聊城。归而言鲁连，欲爵之。鲁连逃隐於海上，曰：“吾与富贵而诎於人，宁贫贱而轻世肆志焉。”

When the general of Yan saw Lu Lian's letter he wept for three days, wavering and unable to decide. He wished to return to Yan, but there was already a breach and he feared execution; he wished to surrender to Qi, but he had killed and captured very many of Qi's men and feared that after surrendering he would be humiliated. With a deep sigh he said: "Rather than let another put the blade to me, I will put it to myself." And he killed himself. Liaocheng fell into disorder, and Tian Dan then put Liaocheng to the sword. On his return he spoke of Lu Lian and wished to ennoble him. Lu Lian fled and hid himself by the sea, saying: "Rather than be rich and honoured and bend to others, I would be poor and lowly, make light of the age and follow my own will."

Terms

the Vertical Alliance / 从; 纵n. the north–south league of the eastern states against Qin.
Prince / 公子n. a son of a ruling king or lord.
jin of gold / 金n. the unit in which sums of gold are reckoned: a yi 镒 under the Warring States and Qin, a jin 斤 under Han.
gentlemen / 士n. men of the gentleman class, from whom officers and retainers were drawn.
Fortress / 城n. a walled place.
the Horizontal Alliance / 连横; 衡n. the policy of individual states allying with Qin, east to west; the opposite of the Vertical.
ten thousand chariots / 万乘n. the measure of a great state's strength; a ruler of ten thousand chariots is a king.
the Lords / 诸侯npl. the rulers of the states, collectively; in this period the kings of the warring states and the lesser lords, as distinct from the Zhou king.
the Five Hegemons / 五霸; 五伯n. the five lords who in turn led the states in the Spring and Autumn period.
the Three Kings / 三王n. the founders of the Xia, Shang and Zhou.

Persons

Prince Wuji of Wei 魏公子无忌Youngest son of King Zhao of Wei, enfeoffed as Lord Xinling 信陵君.
Jin Bi 晋鄙Wei general.
Lord Pingyuan 平原君Zhao Sheng 赵胜, younger brother of King Huiwen of Zhao; his death is placed in the fourteenth year of King Xiaocheng (252 BC) by the Hereditary House of Zhao and in the fifteenth (251 BC) by the Table.
Lu Zhonglian 鲁仲连A gentleman of Qi, the persuader who refused all reward; the Biography calls him Lu Lian 鲁连.
King Xi of Yan 燕王喜King of Yan from 254 BC; son of King Xiao.
Li Fu 栗腹Chancellor of Yan (the Suoyin: a surname and personal name; the Chancellor of Yan).
Mo Di 墨翟Mozi, the philosopher; the Zhengyi recalls his defence of Song against Chu.
Sun Bin 孙膑General and strategist of Qi under King Wei.
Guan Zhong 管仲Guan Yiwu 管夷吾, Chancellor to Duke Huan of Qi; formerly Tutor to Prince Jiu.
Duke Huan of Qi 齐桓公Xiaobai 小白, Duke of Qi, reigned 685–643 BC, first of the Five Hegemons.
Prince Jiu of Qi 公子纠Prince of Qi, rival of his brother Xiaobai for the dukedom, killed by Lu in 685 BC; Guan Zhong had been his Tutor.
Cao Mo 曹沫General of Lu under Duke Zhuang, who held Duke Huan of Qi at dagger-point at the covenant of Ke (681 BC) and recovered Lu's lost land; the Suoyin writes 曹昧.
Duke Zhuang of Lu 鲁庄公Duke of Lu, reigned 693–662 BC; the lord of Lu of Cao Mo's story.

Chu 楚the state of Chu, with its seat at Juyang.
Yan 燕the state of Yan, with its capital at Ji (modern Beijing).

Liaocheng 聊城a Qi city on the lower Yellow River; a County of Bozhou in the names of the Tang (Zhengyi); modern Liaocheng, Shandong.
Qin 秦the state of Qin, with its capital at Xianyang, Shaanxi.
Zhao 赵the state of Zhao, with its capital at Handan, Hebei; one of the three states formed from Jin.

Notes

1 twenty odd years afterThe rescue of Zhao was in 257 BC (told under 256 BC, §4), so "twenty-odd years after" would fall in the 230s; but the Jijie on the passage cites Xu Guang, by the Table, placing Tian Dan's attack on Liaocheng ten-odd years after Changping (260 BC), that is in the 240s, and the Suoyin notes that the "thirty-odd years" of some texts is an error. The compiler enters the three items here with their date left open.
2 It happened just thenThe passage follows Lu Zhonglian's refusal to let Zhao honour Qin as Emperor during the siege of Handan; the relief of Zhao is told under 256 BC (§4).
3 Tian Dan of Qi attacked LiaochengThe Jijie cites Xu Guang: by the Table, Tian Dan's attack on Liaocheng is ten-odd years after Changping. The Suoyin: Xu Guang, following the Table, puts it ten-odd years after Changping; the reading "thirty-odd years" is wrong. The Zhengyi on Liaocheng: now a County of Bozhou, in the names of the Tang.
4 shun death却死: the Suoyin: to avoid death.
5 Qi has no mind to turn southThe Suoyin on Qi's Nanyang: Qi's lands north of the Huai and on the Si; on Pinglu: a town on the western border, and the Zhengyi: a County of Yanzhou in the names of the Tang; on Jibei: the land of Liaocheng. The Zhengyi: Qi has no mind to attack Chu and Wei to the south, holding the harm at Nanyang and Pinglu small beside the gain of Liaocheng, which it will surely attack.
6 the Horizontal Alliance with Qin is formed衡秦之势成: the Suoyin: Qin and Qi were at peace at this time, hence the phrase.
7 gives up NanyangThe Suoyin: Qi gives up the lands on the Si that Chu attacked, cuts off the right-hand land that Wei attacked, Pinglu, leaving it unrelieved, and bends its purpose to attacking Liaocheng and settling Jibei.
8 Chu and Wei have both withdrawn交退: the Suoyin: 交 is "both"; earlier Chu attacked Nanyang and Wei Pinglu; now both armies have withdrawn and Yan's relief does not come, so the position is perilous.
9 Li FuThe Jijie cites Xu Guang: this event is ten years from Changping. Li Fu's defeat by Zhao and the siege of Yan are told under 251 BC.
10 the defence of Mo DiThe Zhengyi: as Mo Di defended Song and repulsed the Chu army.
11 the troops of Sun BinThe Zhengyi: Sun Bin could so comfort his soldiers that they had no second thoughts.
12 furnish the persuaders资说士: the Suoyin: having nourished the people, he also furnishes the persuaders, aiming in the end at a strong state; Liu reads 说士 as 锐士, "crack troops", which is plausible but it is better to keep the character.
13 set the state right矫国更俗: the Suoyin: he wishes the general of Yan to return to Yan, set the state's affairs right and reform its corrupt customs.
14 Or if you have no mind for that亡意: the Suoyin: 亡 is read wu, "none": if you have no mind at all to return to Yan, then abandon Yan and go east to Qi.
15 Tao and Wey陶、卫: the Suoyin: Yan Du's note on the Zhanguoce takes Tao as Tao Zhu Gong and Wey as Prince Jing of Wey, wrongly; Wang Shao: Wei Ran was enfeoffed at Tao and Lord Shang was surnamed Wey, and the comparison is with these. Neither is tagged, the names standing for the men.
16 call yourselves lords称孤: 孤, "the orphan", is the self-designation of the ruler of a state; to call oneself so is to rule.
17 abandoned Prince Jiu遗公子纠: the Suoyin: 遗 is to abandon, abandoning Prince Jiu to serve Xiaobai. The Zhengyi: Guan Zhong was Tutor to Prince Jiu, whom Lu killed; not following him in death was cowardice.
18 Slaves and bondwomen臧获: the Jijie cites the Fangyan: between Jing and the Huai, the sea and Mount Dai, Yan and Qi, a male slave is abused as 臧 and a female as 获.
19 the first of the Five HegemonsThe Zhengyi: Duke Huan of Qi was the first to receive from King Xiang of Zhou the sacrificial meat of Kings Wen and Wu, the red bow and arrows and the great carriage, hence first of the Five Hegemons.
20 Master Cao曹子: the Suoyin: the Lu general Cao Mei 曹昧, that is Cao Mo 曹沫. His three defeats and the covenant of Ke fall under Duke Zhuang of Lu, 681 BC.
21 held it to Duke Huan s breast枝桓公之心: the Suoyin: 枝 is 拟, to point at.
