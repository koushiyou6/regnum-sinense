Shiji IV: Biographies

Shiji 史记 · 247 BC · 秦庄襄王三年

Introduction

The second half of the Biography of the Prince of Wei, Lord Xinling: his ten years in Zhao, his return to command the five states against Qin in the thirtieth year of King Anxi, the slander that undid him, and his death; told in full under 256 BC and repeated here for the year of the victory at Hewai.

[§4] Shiji IV: Biographies

魏公子列传：魏王怒公子之盗其兵符，矫杀晋鄙，公子亦自知也。已卻秦存赵，使将将其军归魏，而公子独与客留赵。赵孝成王德公子之矫夺晋鄙兵而存赵，乃与平原君计，以五城封公子。公子闻之，意骄矜而有自功之色。客有说公子曰：“物有不可忘，或有不可不忘。夫人有德於公子，公子不可忘也；公子有德於人，原公子忘之也。且矫魏王令，夺晋鄙兵以救赵，於赵则有功矣，於魏则未为忠臣也。公子乃自骄而功之，窃为公子不取也。”於是公子立自责，似若无所容者。赵王埽除自迎，执主人之礼，引公子就西阶。公子侧行辞让，从东阶上。自言罪过，以负於魏，无功於赵。赵王侍酒至暮，口不忍献五城，以公子退让也。公子竟留赵。赵王以鄗为公子汤沐邑，魏亦复以信陵奉公子。公子留赵。

Biography of the Prince of Wei: The King of Wei was angry that the Prince1 had stolen his army tally and killed Jin Bi under a feigned command, and the Prince knew it himself. Having driven back Qin and preserved Zhao, he had a general lead his army back to Wei, while the Prince alone stayed in Zhao with his retainers. King Xiaocheng of Zhao, grateful to the Prince for seizing Jin Bi's army under a feigned command and preserving Zhao, planned with Lord Pingyuan to enfeoff the Prince with five Fortresses. When the Prince heard of it, his mind grew proud and he wore the look of one who takes credit for his deed. One of his retainers said to the Prince: "There are things that must not be forgotten, and things that must not be remembered. When another has done the Prince a kindness, the Prince must not forget it; when the Prince has done another a kindness, I would have the Prince forget it. Moreover, to feign the King of Wei's command and seize Jin Bi's army to relieve Zhao was a service to Zhao, but towards Wei it was not the act of a loyal subject. For the Prince to grow proud and take credit for it — I privately think the Prince should not." At this the Prince at once reproached himself, as if there were nowhere he could hide. The King of Zhao swept the way and came to meet him in person, performed the rites of a host, and led the Prince towards the western steps. The Prince stepped aside in refusal and went up by the eastern steps. He declared his own fault: he had failed Wei and done no service to Zhao. The King of Zhao kept him at wine until dusk, and could not bring himself to offer the five Fortresses, because of the Prince's self-abasement. In the end the Prince stayed in Zhao. The King of Zhao gave the Prince Hao as his private estate, and Wei too restored Xinling for the Prince's support. The Prince remained in Zhao.

公子闻赵有处士毛公藏於博徒，薛公藏於卖浆家，公子欲见两人，两人自匿不肯见公子。公子闻所在，乃间步往从此两人游，甚欢。平原君闻之，谓其夫人曰：“始吾闻夫人弟公子天下无双，今吾闻之，乃妄从博徒卖浆者游，公子妄人耳。”夫人以告公子。公子乃谢夫人去，曰：“始吾闻平原君贤，故负魏王而救赵，以称平原君。平原君之游，徒豪举耳，不求士也。无忌自在大梁时，常闻此两人贤，至赵，恐不得见。以无忌从之游，尚恐其不我欲也，今平原君乃以为羞，其不足从游。”乃装为去。夫人具以语平原君。平原君乃免冠谢，固留公子。平原君门下闻之，半去平原君归公子，天下士复往归公子，公子倾平原君客。

The Prince heard that in Zhao there were gentlemen in retirement: Master Mao, hidden among the gamblers, and Master Xue, hidden in the house of a seller of drinks. The Prince wished to see the two men, but the two hid themselves and would not see the Prince. When the Prince learned where they were, he went secretly on foot to keep company with the two, and they were very happy together. When Lord Pingyuan heard of it, he said to his wife: "At first I heard that your brother the Prince had no equal under Heaven; now I hear that he recklessly keeps company with gamblers and sellers of drinks — the Prince is a reckless man, that is all." His wife told the Prince. The Prince then took leave of his sister to go, saying: "At first I heard that Lord Pingyuan was a man of worth, and so I betrayed the King of Wei to relieve Zhao, to answer Lord Pingyuan's expectations. But Lord Pingyuan's company is only a grand display; he does not seek gentlemen. When Wuji was still in Daliang he often heard that these two were worthy men, and on coming to Zhao he feared he would not get to see them. With Wuji keeping their company, I still feared they would not want me; now Lord Pingyuan thinks it a disgrace — he is not worth keeping company with." And he packed to leave. His wife told Lord Pingyuan everything. Lord Pingyuan then took off his cap, apologised, and insisted that the Prince stay. When Lord Pingyuan's household heard of it, half of them left Lord Pingyuan and went over to the Prince, and the gentlemen of all under Heaven went over to the Prince once more; the Prince drew away Lord Pingyuan's retainers.

公子留赵十年不归。秦闻公子在赵，日夜出兵东伐魏。魏王患之，使使往请公子。公子恐其怒之，乃诫门下：“有敢为魏王使通者，死。”宾客皆背魏之赵，莫敢劝公子归。毛公、薛公两人往见公子曰：“公子所以重於赵，名闻诸侯者，徒以有魏也。今秦攻魏，魏急而公子不恤，使秦破大梁而夷先王之宗庙，公子当何面目立天下乎？”语未及卒，公子立变色，告车趣驾归救魏。

The Prince stayed in Zhao ten years without returning. Qin, hearing that the Prince was in Zhao, sent troops east day and night to attack Wei. The King of Wei was troubled by this and sent envoys to ask the Prince back. The Prince, fearing that the King was angry with him, warned his household: "Whoever dares to carry word for the King of Wei's envoys dies." His guests had all turned their backs on Wei for Zhao, and none dared urge the Prince to return. The two men Master Mao and Master Xue went to see the Prince and said: "The reason the Prince weighs heavily in Zhao and his name is heard among the Lords is solely that there is a Wei. Now Qin attacks Wei; Wei is in extremity and the Prince does not care. Should Qin destroy Daliang and level the ancestral temple of the former kings, with what face could the Prince stand before all under Heaven?" Before they had finished speaking the Prince's colour changed at once; he called for his carriage and hurried home to relieve Wei.

魏王见公子，相与泣，而以上将军印授公子，公子遂将。魏安釐王三十年，公子使使遍告诸侯。诸侯闻公子将，各遣将将兵救魏。公子率五国之兵破秦军於河外，走蒙骜。遂乘胜逐秦军至函谷关，抑秦兵，秦兵不敢出。当是时，公子威振天下，诸侯之客进兵法，公子皆名之，故世俗称魏公子兵法。

The King of Wei saw the Prince, and they wept together; and he gave the seal of Supreme General to the Prince, and the Prince took command. In the thirtieth year of King Anxi of Wei2 the Prince sent envoys to inform all the Lords. When the Lords heard that the Prince was in command, each sent generals with troops to relieve Wei. The Prince led the troops of five states and defeated the Qin army at Hewai, putting Meng Ao to flight. Then, following up the victory, he pursued the Qin army to Hangu Pass and held the Qin troops in check, so that the Qin troops dared not come out. At this time the Prince's majesty shook all under Heaven, and the retainers of the Lords presented methods of war, to all of which the Prince gave his name; hence the world at large calls them the Prince of Wei's Methods of War.

秦王患之，乃行金万斤於魏，求晋鄙客，令毁公子於魏王曰：“公子亡在外十年矣，今为魏将，诸侯将皆属，诸侯徒闻魏公子，不闻魏王。公子亦欲因此时定南面而王，诸侯畏公子之威，方欲共立之。”秦数使反间，伪贺公子得立为魏王未也。魏王日闻其毁，不能不信，後果使人代公子将。公子自知再以毁废，乃谢病不朝，与宾客为长夜饮，饮醇酒，多近妇女。日夜为乐饮者四岁，竟病酒而卒。其岁，魏安釐王亦薨。

The King of Qin3 was troubled by this, and sent ten thousand jin of gold into Wei to seek out Jin Bi's former retainers and have them slander the Prince to the King of Wei: "The Prince was ten years in exile abroad; now he is Wei's general, and the generals of the Lords are all under him; the Lords hear only of the Prince of Wei and not of the King of Wei. The Prince also wishes to seize this moment to face south and make himself king; the Lords, in awe of the Prince's majesty, are about to set him up together." Qin several times sent agents of discord, feigning to congratulate the Prince on whether he had yet been set up as King of Wei. The King of Wei heard the slander daily and could not help believing it, and afterwards did indeed send someone to replace the Prince in command. The Prince, knowing that he had a second time been cast aside through slander, pleaded illness and did not attend court; with his guests he drank the night through, drinking strong wine and keeping much company with women. Day and night he made merry with drink for four years, and in the end died of drink. That same year King Anxi of Wei also died.

秦闻公子死，使蒙骜攻魏，拔二十城，初置东郡。其後秦稍蚕食魏，十八岁而虏魏王，屠大梁。

When Qin heard that the Prince was dead, it sent Meng Ao to attack Wei; he took twenty Fortresses, and Dong Commandery was first established. Thereafter Qin ate away at Wei little by little, as a silkworm eats a leaf, and in eighteen years it captured the King of Wei and slaughtered Daliang.

高祖始微少时，数闻公子贤。及即天子位，每过大梁，常祠公子。高祖十二年，从击黥布还，为公子置守冢五家，世世岁以四时奉祠公子。

When Gaozu was still young and humble, he often heard of the Prince's worth. After he ascended the throne as Son of Heaven, whenever he passed Daliang he always sacrificed to the Prince. In the twelfth year of Gaozu, returning from the attack on Qing Bu, he established five tomb-keeping households for the Prince, to sacrifice to the Prince at the four seasons every year, generation after generation.

太史公曰：吾过大梁之墟，求问其所谓夷门。夷门者，城之东门也。天下诸公子亦有喜士者矣，然信陵君之接岩穴隐者，不耻下交，有以也。名冠诸侯，不虚耳。高祖每过之而令民奉祠不绝也。

The Grand Historian says: I passed the ruins of Daliang and sought out what is called the Yi Gate. The Yi Gate was the eastern gate of the Fortress. Among the princes of all under Heaven there have been others who delighted in gentlemen, but that Lord Xinling received recluses of the caves and cliffs, and was not ashamed to associate with those beneath him, had its reason. That his name stood first among the Lords was no empty thing. And Gaozu, whenever he passed, had the people sacrifice to him without cease.

Terms

Fortress / 城n. a walled place.
private estate / 汤沐邑n. literally a bath-and-ablution town: a town whose revenue was assigned to a person for private expenses.
gentlemen in retirement / 处士n. gentlemen of worth living in private, without office.
Supreme General / 上将军n. the commander-in-chief.
the Lords / 诸侯npl. the rulers of the states, collectively; in this period the kings of the warring states and the lesser lords, as distinct from the Zhou king.
agents of discord / 反间n. agents sent to sow division at an enemy court.
Son of Heaven / 天子n. the emperor.
tomb-keeping households / 守冢n. households assigned to tend a tomb and keep up its sacrifices.
the Grand Historian / 太史公n. Sima Qian's own title, introducing his appraisal at the end of a chapter.
