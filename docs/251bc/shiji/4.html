[§4] Shiji IV: Biographies

平原君虞卿列传：平原君赵胜者，赵之诸公子也。诸子中胜最贤，喜宾客，宾客盖至者数千人。平原君相赵惠文王及孝成王，三去相，三复位，封於东武城。

Biography of Lord Pingyuan and Yu Qing: Lord Pingyuan, Zhao Sheng, was one of the Princes of Zhao. Among the princes Sheng was the worthiest; he was fond of retainers, and the retainers who came to him numbered some several thousand. Lord Pingyuan was Chancellor to King Huiwen of Zhao and to King Xiaocheng; three times he left the chancellorship and three times he was restored to it. He was enfeoffed at Dongwucheng.

平原君家楼临民家。民家有躄者，槃散行汲。平原君美人居楼上，临见，大笑之。明日，躄者至平原君门，请曰：“臣闻君之喜士，士不远千里而至者，以君能贵士而贱妾也。臣不幸有罢癃之病，而君之後宫临而笑臣，臣原得笑臣者头。”平原君笑应曰：“诺。”躄者去，平原君笑曰：“观此竖子，乃欲以一笑之故杀吾美人，不亦甚乎！”终不杀。居岁馀，宾客门下舍人稍稍引去者过半。平原君怪之，曰：“胜所以待诸君者未尝敢失礼，而去者何多也？”门下一人前对曰：“以君之不杀笑躄者，以君为爱色而贱士，士即去耳。”於是平原君乃斩笑躄者美人头，自造门进躄者，因谢焉。其後门下乃复稍稍来。是时齐有孟尝，魏有信陵，楚有春申，故争相倾以待士。

A storeyed building of Lord Pingyuan's house overlooked the house of a commoner. In the commoner's house there was a lame man, who hobbled1 out to draw water. A beauty of Lord Pingyuan's who lived in the upper storey looked down, saw him and laughed loudly at him. The next day the lame man came to Lord Pingyuan's gate and begged: "Your servant has heard that you, my lord, are fond of gentlemen, and that gentlemen do not think a thousand li too far to come to you, because you are able to honour gentlemen and hold women cheap. Your servant has the misfortune to be afflicted with a crooked back2, and a woman of your rear palace looked down and laughed at me. I beg to be given the head of the one who laughed at me." Lord Pingyuan laughed and answered: "Agreed." When the lame man had gone, Lord Pingyuan laughed and said: "Look at this fellow: for the sake of a single laugh he wants to kill my beauty. Is that not going too far?" In the end he did not kill her. A year or more passed, and more than half of his retainers and the household retainers under his gate drifted away. Lord Pingyuan thought it strange and said: "In my treatment of you gentlemen I, Sheng, have never dared to fail in courtesy; why then are so many leaving?" One of those under his gate came forward and answered: "Because you did not kill the one who laughed at the lame man, they take you for one who loves beauty and holds gentlemen cheap, and so the gentlemen leave." Thereupon Lord Pingyuan cut off the head of the beauty who had laughed at the lame man, went in person to his door, presented it to the lame man, and apologised to him. After that those under his gate gradually came back. At that time Qi had Mengchang, Wei had Xinling and Chu had Chunshen, and so they competed to outdo one another in receiving3 gentlemen.

秦之围邯郸，赵使平原君求救，合从於楚，约与食客门下有勇力文武备具者二十人偕。平原君曰：“使文能取胜，则善矣。文不能取胜，则歃血於华屋之下，必得定从而还。士不外索，取於食客门下足矣。”得十九人，馀无可取者，无以满二十人。门下有毛遂者，前，自赞於平原君曰：“遂闻君将合从於楚，约与食客门下二十人偕，不外索。今少一人，原君即以遂备员而行矣。”平原君曰：“先生处胜之门下几年於此矣？”毛遂曰：“三年於此矣。”平原君曰：“夫贤士之处世也，譬若锥之处囊中，其末立见。今先生处胜之门下三年於此矣，左右未有所称诵，胜未有所闻，是先生无所有也。先生不能，先生留。”毛遂曰：“臣乃今日请处囊中耳。使遂蚤得处囊中，乃颖脱而出，非特其末见而已。”平原君竟与毛遂偕。十九人相与目笑之而未废也。

When Qin besieged Handan, Zhao sent Lord Pingyuan to seek relief and to join the Vertical Alliance with Chu; he arranged to take with him twenty of the retainers under his gate who had courage and strength and were complete in both civil and martial accomplishments. Lord Pingyuan said: "If the victory can be won by civil means, very good. If it cannot be won by civil means, then we shall smear the blood beneath the splendid hall, and I shall without fail settle the Alliance before I return. Gentlemen are not to be sought outside; to take them from the retainers under my gate will suffice." He found nineteen, and among the rest there was none fit to take, so he had no way to make up the twenty. Among those under his gate was one Mao Sui, who came forward and commended himself to Lord Pingyuan: "Sui has heard that you, my lord, are about to join the Vertical Alliance with Chu and have arranged to take twenty of the retainers under your gate with you, not seeking outside. Now you are one man short; I beg you, my lord, to make up the number with Sui and set out." Lord Pingyuan said: "How many years, sir, have you been under my gate?" Mao Sui said: "Three years now." Lord Pingyuan said: "A worthy gentleman's place in the world is like an awl's place in a bag: its point shows at once. Now you, sir, have been under my gate three years, and those about me have had nothing to say in your praise, nor have I heard anything of you; that means you have nothing in you, sir. You are not able, sir; you will stay." Mao Sui said: "It is only today that your servant asks to be put in the bag. Had Sui been put in the bag earlier, the whole of him would have come through4, not merely his point." In the end Lord Pingyuan took Mao Sui with him. The nineteen exchanged glances and smiled at him, but did not yet cast him off5.

毛遂比至楚，与十九人论议，十九人皆服。平原君与楚合从，言其利害，日出而言之，日中不决。十九人谓毛遂曰：“先生上。”毛遂按剑历阶而上，谓平原君曰：“从之利害，两言而决耳。今日出而言从，日中不决，何也？”楚王谓平原君曰：“客何为者也？”平原君曰：“是胜之舍人也。”楚王叱曰：“胡不下！吾乃与而君言，汝何为者也！”毛遂按剑而前曰：“王之所以叱遂者，以楚国之众也。今十步之内，王不得恃楚国之众也，王之命县於遂手。吾君在前，叱者何也？且遂闻汤以七十里之地王天下，文王以百里之壤而臣诸侯，岂其士卒众多哉，诚能据其势而奋其威。今楚地方五千里，持戟百万，此霸王之资也。以楚之彊，天下弗能当。白起，小竖子耳，率数万之众，兴师以与楚战，一战而举鄢郢，再战而烧夷陵，三战而辱王之先人。此百世之怨而赵之所羞，而王弗知恶焉。合从者为楚，非为赵也。吾君在前，叱者何也？”楚王曰：“唯唯，诚若先生之言，谨奉社稷而以从。”毛遂曰：“从定乎？”楚王曰：“定矣。”毛遂谓楚王之左右曰：“取鸡狗马之血来。”毛遂奉铜槃而跪进之楚王曰：“王当歃血而定从，次者吾君，次者遂。”遂定从於殿上。毛遂左手持槃血而右手招十九人曰：“公相与歃此血於堂下。公等录录，所谓因人成事者也。”

By the time Mao Sui reached Chu he had debated with the nineteen, and all nineteen submitted to him. Lord Pingyuan treated with Chu for the Vertical Alliance, setting out its advantages and disadvantages; he spoke of it from sunrise, and by midday nothing was decided. The nineteen said to Mao Sui: "Go up, sir." Mao Sui, his hand on his sword, mounted the steps and went up, and said to Lord Pingyuan: "The advantages and disadvantages of the Alliance can be decided in two words. Today you have spoken of the Alliance since sunrise and by midday it is not decided; why is that?" The King of Chu said to Lord Pingyuan: "What is this guest?" Lord Pingyuan said: "This is a household retainer of mine." The King of Chu shouted at him: "Why do you not go down! I am speaking with your master; what are you!" Mao Sui, his hand on his sword, stepped forward and said: "Your Majesty shouts at Sui because of the multitude of the state of Chu. But now, within these ten paces, Your Majesty cannot rely on the multitude of Chu: Your Majesty's life hangs in Sui's hand. My master is before you; what is this shouting? Moreover, Sui has heard that Tang with seventy li of land became king of all under heaven, and King Wen with a hundred li of soil made the Lords his subjects. Was it that their soldiers were many? It was that they were truly able to hold their position and put forth their might. Now Chu's territory is five thousand li square, and it has a million halberd-bearers: that is the stuff of a hegemon king. With Chu's strength nothing under heaven can withstand it. Bai Qi is a mere stripling, yet at the head of a few tens of thousands he raised an army to fight Chu: in one battle he took Yan and Ying, in a second he burned Yiling, in a third he disgraced Your Majesty's ancestors. That is a grudge for a hundred generations and a thing Zhao is ashamed of on your behalf, yet Your Majesty does not know to hate6 it. The Alliance is for Chu's sake, not for Zhao's. My master is before you; what is this shouting?" The King of Chu said: "Yes, yes. It is truly as you say, sir: I shall reverently commit the altars of soil and grain to the Alliance." Mao Sui said: "Is the Alliance settled?" The King of Chu said: "It is settled." Mao Sui said to the King of Chu's attendants: "Bring the blood of a cock, a dog and a horse7." Mao Sui held up the bronze basin8, knelt and offered it to the King of Chu, saying: "Your Majesty should smear the blood and settle the Alliance; next my master, next Sui." So the Alliance was settled in the upper hall. Mao Sui, holding the basin of blood in his left hand, beckoned with his right to the nineteen and said: "You gentlemen shall smear this blood together below the hall. You, sirs, are mere followers9, what is called accomplishing things through others."

平原君已定从而归，归至於赵，曰：“胜不敢复相士。胜相士多者千人，寡者百数，自以为不失天下之士，今乃於毛先生而失之也。毛先生一至楚，而使赵重於九鼎大吕。毛先生以三寸之舌，强于百万之师。胜不敢复相士。”遂以为上客。

When Lord Pingyuan had settled the Alliance and returned, on reaching Zhao he said: "Sheng will never again dare to judge gentlemen. Of the gentlemen Sheng has judged, at most a thousand, at least some hundreds, I thought I had let no gentleman of the world slip past me; and now in Master Mao I let one slip. Master Mao went once to Chu and made Zhao weightier than the Nine Tripods and the Great Lü10. With a tongue three inches long Master Mao was stronger than an army of a million. Sheng will never again dare to judge gentlemen." And he made him a guest of the first rank.

平原君既返赵，楚使春申君将兵赴救赵，魏信陵君亦矫夺晋鄙军往救赵，皆未至。秦急围邯郸，邯郸急，且降，平原君甚患之。邯郸传舍吏子李同说平原君曰：“君不忧赵亡邪？”平原君曰：“赵亡则胜为虏，何为不忧乎？”李同曰：“邯郸之民，炊骨易子而食，可谓急矣，而君之後宫以百数，婢妾被绮縠，馀粱肉，而民褐衣不完，糟糠不厌。民困兵尽，或剡木为矛矢，而君器物锺磬自若。使秦破赵，君安得有此？使赵得全，君何患无有？今君诚能令夫人以下编於士卒之间，分功而作，家之所有尽散以飨士，士方其危苦之时，易德耳。”於是平原君从之，得敢死之士三千人。李同遂与三千人赴秦军，秦军为之卻三十里。亦会楚、魏救至，秦兵遂罢，邯郸复存。李同战死，封其父为李侯。

When Lord Pingyuan had returned to Zhao, Chu sent Lord Chunshen at the head of troops to hasten to Zhao's relief, and Lord Xinling of Wei too, feigning a command, seized Jin Bi's army and went to Zhao's relief; but neither had yet arrived. Qin pressed the siege of Handan hard; Handan was in straits and about to surrender, and Lord Pingyuan was greatly troubled by it. Li Tong, son of an officer of the post-house of Handan, advised Lord Pingyuan: "Are you not anxious, my lord, that Zhao will perish?" Lord Pingyuan said: "If Zhao perishes, Sheng will be a captive; how could I not be anxious?" Li Tong said: "The people of Handan cook bones for fuel and exchange their children to eat: that may be called straits indeed. Yet your rear palace numbers its women by the hundred, your maids and concubines are clothed in figured silk and gauze and have fine grain and meat to spare, while the people's coarse clothes are in tatters and they cannot get their fill of husks and chaff. The people are exhausted and the weapons used up, some sharpening wood into spears and arrows, while your vessels, your bells and chimes, remain as they were. If Qin breaks Zhao, how will you keep these, my lord? If Zhao comes through whole, what fear have you of lacking them? Now if you, my lord, could truly have your ladies and those below them enrolled among the soldiers, sharing the work according to their tasks, and could disperse all that your house holds to feast the gentlemen: gentlemen in their hour of danger and hardship are easily won by kindness11." Thereupon Lord Pingyuan followed his advice and obtained three thousand gentlemen ready to die. Li Tong then went with the three thousand against the Qin army, and the Qin army fell back thirty li before them. It happened too that the relief from Chu and Wei arrived; the Qin troops then withdrew, and Handan survived. Li Tong died in the fighting, and his father was enfeoffed as Marquis of Li.

虞卿欲以信陵君之存邯郸为平原君请封。公孙龙闻之，夜驾见平原君曰：“龙闻虞卿欲以信陵君之存邯郸为君请封，有之乎？”平原君曰：“然。”龙曰：“此甚不可。且王举君而相赵者，非以君之智能为赵国无有也。割东武城而封君者，非以君为有功也，而以国人无勋，乃以君为亲戚故也。君受相印不辞无能，割地不言无功者，亦自以为亲戚故也。今信陵君存邯郸而请封，是亲戚受城而国人计功也。此甚不可。且虞卿操其两权，事成，操右券以责；事不成，以虚名德君。君必勿听也。”平原君遂不听虞卿。

Yu Qing wished to ask a fief for Lord Pingyuan on the ground that Lord Xinling had saved Handan. When Gongsun Long heard of it, he drove by night to see Lord Pingyuan and said: "Long has heard that Yu Qing wishes to ask a fief for you, my lord, on the ground that Lord Xinling saved Handan. Is it so?" Lord Pingyuan said: "It is." Long said: "This is quite wrong. The King raised you to be Chancellor of Zhao not because there was no one in the state of Zhao with your wisdom and ability; he cut off Dongwucheng and enfeoffed you not because you had merit, nor because the men of the state had no achievements, but because you are his kin. When you accepted the Chancellor's seal you did not decline it as beyond your ability, and when land was cut off for you you did not say you had no merit, because you too held it to be on account of your kinship. Now, when Lord Xinling has saved Handan, to ask for a fief would be for a kinsman to receive a Fortress while the men of the state reckon up merit12. This is quite wrong. Besides, Yu Qing holds both ends: if the affair succeeds, he holds the right-hand tally and claims his due13; if it fails, he puts you in his debt for the empty name of it. You must not listen to him." So Lord Pingyuan did not listen to Yu Qing.

平原君以赵孝成王十五年卒。子孙代，後竟与赵俱亡。

Lord Pingyuan died in the fifteenth year of King Xiaocheng of Zhao14. His sons and grandsons succeeded him, and in the end they perished together with Zhao.

廉颇蔺相如列传：自邯郸围解五年，而燕用栗腹之谋，曰“赵壮者尽於长平，其孤未壮”，举兵击赵。赵使廉颇将，击，大破燕军於鄗，杀栗腹，遂围燕。燕割五城请和，乃听之。赵以尉文封廉颇为信平君，为假相国。

Biography of Lian Po and Lin Xiangru: Five years after the siege of Handan was raised, Yan adopted Li Fu's plan — "those of Zhao in their prime were all used up at Changping, and their orphans are not yet grown" — and raised troops to strike at Zhao. Zhao sent Lian Po in command; he struck and heavily defeated the Yan army at Hao, killed Li Fu, and went on to besiege Yan. Yan ceded five Fortresses and sued for peace, and this was granted. Zhao enfeoffed Lian Po with Weiwen15 as Lord Xinping and made him Acting Chancellor of State.

廉颇之免长平归也，失势之时，故客尽去。及复用为将，客又复至。廉颇曰：“客退矣！”客曰：“吁！君何见之晚也？夫天下以市道交，君有势，我则从君，君无势则去，此固其理也，有何怨乎？”居六年，赵使廉颇伐魏之繁阳，拔之。

When Lian Po had been dismissed at Changping and returned home, in the time when he had lost his position, his old retainers all left. When he was again employed as general, the retainers came back again. Lian Po said: "Retainers, withdraw!" A retainer said: "Ah! How late you are in seeing it, my lord! All under heaven make their friendships as in the market: when you have position I follow you, and when you have none I leave. That is the plain principle of it; what is there to resent?" After six years, Zhao sent Lian Po to attack Wei's Fanyang, and he took it.

Terms

Prince / 公子n. a son of a ruling king or lord.
retainers / 食客; 宾客n. guests kept at a lord's table; the clients of a great house.
Chancellor / 相n. the chief minister of a state.
gentlemen / 士n. men of the gentleman class, from whom officers and retainers were drawn.
household retainer / 舍人n. a retainer attached to a lord's household.
the Vertical Alliance / 从; 纵n. the north–south league of the eastern states against Qin.
smear the blood / 歃血v. to seal a covenant by smearing the lips with the blood of the victim.
the Lords / 诸侯npl. the rulers of the states, collectively; in this period the kings of the warring states and the lesser lords, as distinct from the Zhou king.
altars of soil and grain / 社稷n. the altars of a state's gods of soil and grain, standing for the state itself.
the Nine Tripods / 九鼎n. the nine bronze tripod cauldrons held to have been cast by Yu and passed from dynasty to dynasty as the emblem of legitimate rule; their removal to Qin in 256 BC marks the end of Zhou.
guest of the first rank / 上客n. the highest grade of a lord's retainers.
officer / 吏n. a man in government service below the rank of official; a clerk or functionary.
post-house / 传舍n. a lodging on the roads for travellers on official business.
Marquis / 侯n. the holder of a marquisate, the fief named before the title.
Fortress / 城n. a walled place.
Acting Chancellor of State / 假相国n. the office of Chancellor of State held provisionally.

2 crooked back罢癃: the Suoyin: an ailment of the back, the waist bent and the back humped; Xu Guang: 癃 is an illness.
3 receiving待: Xu Guang: one text reads 得, "winning".
4 the whole颖脱而出: the Suoyin cites Zheng Xuan, 颖 is the ring; the whole awl would come through the bag, down to the ring of its handle.
5 smiledThe Suoyin cites Zheng Xuan: they all looked at him and smiled lightly, but could not yet discard him.
6 hate恶: the Zhengyi gives the pronunciation 烏故反, wu in the falling tone, "to hate".
7 bloodThe Suoyin: the victims of a covenant differ by rank, the Son of Heaven using an ox and a horse, the Lords a dog and a boar, Grand Masters and below a cock; here the blood of the covenant is named in general, hence "the blood of a cock, a dog and a horse".
8 bronze basinThe Suoyin: by the Rites of Zhou a pearl basin would be used.
9 followers录录: the Jijie and the Suoyin give the pronunciation lu; the Suoyin cites the Shuowen, "the look of one who follows along".
10 Great Lü大吕: the Zhengyi: the great bell of the Zhou ancestral temple; the Suoyin: the Nine Tripods and the Great Lü are the treasured vessels of the state, so that Zhao was made weighty in the eyes of the world.
11 easily won易德: the Zhengyi: gentlemen in their hour of danger and hardship easily feel gratitude.
12 kinsmanXu Guang: one text reads 是亲戚受城而以国许人, "for a kinsman to receive a Fortress and to promise the state to others".
13 right tally右券: the Suoyin: if Yu Qing's proposal of a fief succeeds, he holds the right-hand half of the tally with which to claim the return of his favour.
14 fifteenth year251 BC. The Suoyin here says that the Table of the Six States and the Hereditary House both give the fourteenth year, unlike this; the Table as it stands gives the fifteenth, and the Suoyin on the Hereditary House cites it so.
15 Weiwen fiefThe Jijie, Xu Guang: 尉文 is the name of a town; the Suoyin: 信平 is a title; the Hanshu's table has a Marquis Jie of Weiwen 尉文节侯, placed in Nan Commandery, so probably 尉 is an office and 文 a name, and the town that Wen had held in fief was given anew to Po, who was then styled Lord Xinping.
